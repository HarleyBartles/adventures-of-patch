# Adventures frame buster

## Overview

A frame is a teaching machine, not a coat of paint. A green Adventures frame
makes the lesson physically happen through a bounded world, a Patch-led story,
and a visible change that the audience can apply outside the story.

## Owned decision

Decide whether the proposed frame is `green`, `amber`, or `blocked` for the
readiness ladder. A green result must establish:

- a bounded theme/world that can generate scenes rather than decoration;
- a concrete autonomous-agent operating lesson;
- the anti-pattern and positive pattern the audience can recognize;
- Patch's character role and agency, without treating Patch as an agent owner;
- cast-to-function, environment-to-domain, and prop/state mappings;
- a continuity object or state transition;
- ordered story progression and concrete audience recommendations.

## Pressure test

Ask the frame to answer these questions without falling back to generic advice:

1. What does the audience see happening in the world?
2. Which real workflow or decision does each important element stand for?
3. What goes wrong, what changes, and what proves the positive pattern?
4. What does Patch do that makes the lesson legible rather than decorative?
5. What can the audience apply immediately after the story?

If answers are slogans such as “be clearer” or “use AI carefully,” the frame is
not green. If the mapping or story spine is missing, route back to framing work.

## Handoff

Read `playbooks/pre-playbook-adventure-readiness.md` and record the frame basis
in the readiness report or source issue. Include the world, real-world model,
anti-pattern, positive pattern, Patch role, mappings, continuity transition,
audience action, unresolved risks, and the next gate.

When the frame is green, hand off to `adventures-visual-preproduction`. When it
is not green, stop before deck planning or body-slide image generation. Do not
invent missing framing inside a later production stage.

## Hard boundaries

- A visual theme without a teaching mapping is not a frame.
- A process diagram or UI metaphor alone is not a frame.
- Patch remains a character; never introduce Patch as an agent, actor, owner,
  or execution lane.
- Do not spend image credits to discover whether a frame works.
