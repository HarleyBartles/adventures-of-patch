# Licensing map

This repository deliberately uses a mixed-licence model. The goal is to make the project useful and reusable without accidentally claiming rights over third-party material or granting a blanket licence over the Adventures of Patch character and world IP.

## Original project-authored software

Original software authored for this repository is licensed under the MIT License where Harley Bartles holds the rights needed to grant that licence and no more specific file, directory, or upstream notice applies.

See `LICENSES/MIT.txt`.

## Original visual and media assets

Original visual and media assets authored for this repository are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International (CC BY-ND 4.0) where Harley Bartles holds the rights needed to grant that licence and no more specific notice applies.

See `LICENSES/CC-BY-ND-4.0.txt` and the canonical Creative Commons licence at:

https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nd/4.0/

In practical terms, for an asset actually covered by CC BY-ND 4.0:

- you may use it privately;
- you may privately make experiments or adaptations;
- you may copy, display, use, and redistribute the licensed asset in its licensed form, including commercially, subject to the licence conditions and attribution requirements;
- you may make technical format changes where the licence permits them;
- you may not publicly share adapted or transformed versions under that licence.

A simple attribution is preferred where practical:

`Adventures of Patch asset by Harley Bartles - CC BY-ND 4.0`

Include a link to the licence where the publication medium reasonably supports one.

## Adventures of Patch characters, world, stories, and branding

Adventures of Patch names, branding, story identity, and other distinctive world elements are likewise reserved.

The repository does not grant a general right to publish:

- new depictions of Adventures of Patch characters;
- modified or transformed character artwork;
- derivative characters based on those characters or designs;
- new Adventures of Patch stories, adventures, fairytales, or other narrative works using the protected character/world identity;
- merchandise or branding built around the protected character/world identity.

Permission may be granted separately. If you want to publish something transformative using Adventures of Patch character or world IP, ask first.

Private, non-public experimentation is not the concern this reservation is aimed at. The practical boundary is publication and public distribution.

## Other original material

Original prose, narrative-development material, presentations, doctrine, project documentation, and other project-authored material that is not expressly within the MIT or CC BY-ND grants remains all rights reserved unless a more specific notice says otherwise.

This distinction is intentional: publishing the repository does not automatically make every story concept, character bible, world element, or presentation an open derivative-work surface.

## Third-party, vendored, adapted, and upstream-derived material

Third-party material keeps its real licence and attribution requirements. Nothing in the repository-wide MIT or CC BY-ND defaults should be read as relicensing material that Harley Bartles does not have the right to relicense.

This includes vendored software, copied or adapted examples, fonts, templates, libraries, submodules, upstream-derived code or media, and any other material carrying its own copyright or licence terms.

More specific notices always win over repository defaults.

See `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` for the repository-level audit surface. An on-disk provenance/licensing pass should continue to record file- and subtree-specific exceptions there and through SPDX/REUSE-compatible metadata where appropriate.

## Machine-readable licensing

The intended end state is for repository licensing to be mechanically inspectable as well as readable by humans. File- and subtree-level SPDX/REUSE classification should be added where appropriate, especially for mixed-licence directories, binary assets, vendored material, and upstream-derived files.

Until that audit is complete, do not infer that a repository-wide default overrides a more specific notice or upstream licence.
