# Bit, Bot and Patch Canonical Costume Style Bible

**Project:** *Adventures of Patch*  
**Adventure / issue context:** Issue #56, *Patch and the Tournament of Reasonable Defaults*  
**Asset pack:** Bit, Bot and Patch in adventure canonical costume  
**Source basis:** Derived from the attached image set in this conversation. This document has not been freshly repo-verified in this pass.

---

## 1. Purpose

This style bible defines the reusable visual language for Patch, Bit and Bot when they appear in their Issue #56 tournament costumes. It is intended to keep character art consistent across story scenes, prompt boards, source-asset generation, asset sheets, diagrams, slides and continuity references.

The costume set belongs to a cheerful, old-fashioned athletic tournament world. The characters should feel like they have been issued participant gear for a formal but friendly event: race bibs, team colour accents, practical shoes, warm-up accessories and simple props that support tournament logic.

The costume language must preserve each character’s identity first, then layer the adventure-specific athletic costume over that identity.

---

## 2. Overall Style

The set uses a clean, high-readability editorial illustration style with simplified forms, rounded silhouette language and light painterly shading. The linework is black or dark grey, confident, smooth and moderately thick. Shapes are readable at small sizes, with no excessive micro-detail.

Characters are presented as clean source assets on a plain white or near-white background. Shadows are soft and minimal, usually a pale grey ground mark or faint cast shadow. The assets are designed for reuse and isolation rather than full environmental storytelling.

The finish should sit between mascot character design, children’s editorial illustration and clean vector-adjacent adventure art. It should not become gritty, overly rendered, photorealistic, anime-overcomplicated or metallic sci-fi realism.

---

## 3. Shared Tournament Costume Language

All three characters wear visible participant race bibs. The bibs are white rectangles with a coloured name band at the top and bold black numerals beneath. The number system is part of the continuity: Patch and Bot use **042**, while Bit uses **007**.

Bibs are pinned, taped or strapped on in a slightly handmade event way. Small corner pin marks, tape tabs or string details are acceptable. They should remain readable and centred on the torso unless the pose naturally shifts them.

Costumes use athletic warm-up logic: soft headbands, sneakers or sport shoes, simple race marks, practical bags, notebooks, hazard tape or start-line props. The costume layer should feel playful but functional.

The palette is character-coded:

- Patch: teal, cream, off-white, dark navy and black accessory accents.
- Bit: white, black and red, with orange hazard accents when needed.
- Bot: white, black and strong blue, with tan-and-blue oversized shoes.

The characters should not be recoloured into each other’s palettes. Their colour identities are part of the canonical costume read.

## 4. Patch: Canonical Tournament Costume

Patch is a small, rounded, soft-bodied mascot-like figure with a pale simple face, black oval eyes and a small mouth. Patch wears a teal hooded outfit with two antenna-like stalks ending in dark round tips. The hood shape is rounded and snug, with a cream-and-teal striped athletic headband wrapped around the forehead.

Patch’s race outfit consists of a cream or off-white sleeveless athletic vest over teal clothing, teal shorts, dark navy leg coverings or joggers, and teal sneakers with white toe caps and soles. The silhouette is compact and childlike: rounded head, short limbs, soft mitten-like hands and simple feet.

The race bib should read **PATCH 042**. The top name band is teal. The numerals are black and bold. The bib is pinned to the vest and may bend slightly with Patch’s body.

Patch carries a small black crossbody bag with a simple terminal-style mark, usually a white `>_` symbol and a tiny teal dot. The strap crosses diagonally across the torso. This bag is an important continuity accessory and should generally remain present unless the scene specifically requires otherwise.

Patch’s expressions are simple but meaningful. Use tiny mouth changes and head/body posture to communicate emotion: mild smile, thoughtful frown, curious tilt, focused note-taking, or uncertain start-line hesitation. Patch should read as observant, careful, curious and quietly competent.

Typical Patch poses include standing neutrally, writing in a spiral notebook, reading race rules, thinking with one hand near the chin, or standing at the start line with a quizzical expression.

Patch must not be made human, given detailed facial features, turned into a conventional animal mascot, or given aggressive athletic bravado. Patch is small, thoughtful and gentle.

## 5. Bit: Canonical Tournament Costume

Bit is a compact wheeled robot with a white shell, black internal structure and red accent system. The head is rounded with a glossy black face screen. Eyes and mouth are red digital marks. The antenna is a single red-tipped stalk on top of the head.

Bit’s body uses a squat, energetic silhouette: large rounded head, compact torso, short arms, black mechanical hands, and a two-wheel lower body. Red circular joints and red trim appear at the ears, shoulders, wrists, torso plate and wheels.

Bit’s race bib should read **BIT 007**. The top name band is red. The numerals are black and bold. The bib is taped or pinned to the torso and may look slightly scruffy, especially in action scenes.

Bit’s personality in costume is mischievous, fast and slightly chaotic. The asset set supports both neutral/hero pose and comedic rule-breaking energy. Bit may burst through hazard tape, lean forward as if racing, or stand in a determined wheeled-run stance. Motion lines and torn tape pieces are acceptable when Bit is in action.

Hazard props associated with Bit include orange cones, red-and-white hazard tape, torn barrier tape, and “do not cross” style warnings. These should be comic and non-threatening, not dangerous or violent.

Bit should not be redesigned as a sleek realistic robot, a drone, a humanoid android or a menacing machine. Keep the toy-like, rounded, expressive robot body and red accent language.

## 6. Bot: Canonical Tournament Costume

Bot is a taller wheeled robot with a white shell, black internal structure and strong blue accent system. The head is rounded with a glossy black face screen. Eyes and mouth are blue digital marks. The antenna is a single blue-tipped stalk on top of the head.

Bot’s silhouette is upright and polite, with a larger body than Bit, rounded shoulder armour, black articulated arms, hand-like black fingers, and a balanced two-wheel base. Bot has a calm, helpful, slightly earnest stage presence.

Bot’s race bib should read **BOT 042**. The top name band is blue. The numerals are black and bold. The bib is mounted over a white mesh-like athletic vest or torso covering.

Bot wears a white-and-blue athletic headband around the top of the head and blue wrist sweatbands around the forearms. Bot also wears oversized tan race shoes strapped awkwardly around the wheels with rope laces. This is a key comic costume detail: the shoes are clearly not natural robot footwear, but they are worn earnestly as tournament equipment.

Bot’s shoes are tan with blue line accents and blue laces. They sit around or beside the wheel base rather than replacing the wheels entirely. They should look securely tied on but inherently impractical.

Bot’s expression language is digital and minimal. Common reads include cheerful closed-eye smile, neutral attentive face, mild concern, or focused stretching. Bot may point, gesture helpfully, stand upright, or stretch like a runner before the event.

Bot should not be depicted without wheels, without the blue accent system, or as a sleek humanoid robot. The awkward shoe-on-wheel gag is part of the costume continuity and should be preserved whenever Bot appears in this costume set.

## 7. Character Relationship and Scale

Patch is the soft-bodied protagonist and should feel smaller and more organic than both robots. Bit is the smaller, faster, red-coded robot. Bot is the taller, blue-coded robot with a more orderly and helpful presence.

The trio should preserve clear silhouette differences:

- Patch: round hood, antennae, soft hands, sneakers, crossbody bag.
- Bit: compact wheeled robot, red accents, single antenna, energetic stance.
- Bot: taller wheeled robot, blue accents, headband, wristbands, oversized tied-on shoes.

Do not homogenize the trio into one robot type or one mascot type. The adventure costume unifies them through bibs and athletic gear, but their base identities remain distinct.

## 8. Props and Accessory Continuity

Patch accessories:

- Black crossbody terminal bag with `>_` marking.
- Spiral notebook and pencil for note-taking.
- Race rules card with teal header and checkboxes.
- Start-line floor mark, when relevant.

Bit accessories:

- Hazard tape or “do not cross” tape.
- Orange cones.
- Torn tape fragments.
- Red race line or speed mark.

Bot accessories:

- Oversized tan-and-blue shoes tied to wheels.
- Blue-and-white headband.
- Blue wristbands.
- Simple ground/start line marks.

Props must remain simple, iconic and readable. Avoid clutter around the characters unless the scene specifically needs it.

---

## 9. Typography and Graphic Marks

Bib text should be bold, high contrast and legible. Use clean block capitals for names and large numerals for race numbers.

Preferred bib structure:

```text
[COLOUR NAME BAND]
PATCH / BIT / BOT
[WHITE NUMBER FIELD]
042 / 007
```

The race-rules card may include short readable lines such as “RACE RULES,” “BE CURIOUS,” “BE RELIABLE,” “BE ORGANIZED,” and “BE HELPFUL,” but small text should not be overused. When text accuracy matters, keep it large and simple.

Avoid gibberish marks on central continuity items where the story meaning depends on the sign or label. Decorative microtext can be abstract, but bibs, names, numbers and major rule-card headings should remain readable.

---

## 10. Pose and Expression Guidelines

Patch poses should emphasize careful interpretation: looking, checking, writing, thinking, cautiously starting. Patch can be curious, puzzled or gently pleased, but not smug or aggressive.

Bit poses should emphasize kinetic comedy: racing forward, bursting through tape, pumping fists, or pausing in a determined stance. Bit can be chaotic, but still cute and friendly.

Bot poses should emphasize helpfulness and earnest compliance: pointing, presenting, standing at attention, stretching before the race, or looking mildly worried when something seems wrong.

Facial expression is intentionally simple across all three characters. Keep emotional reads clear through posture, gesture and screen/face shape rather than complex facial anatomy.

---

## 11. Lighting and Rendering

Use bright studio-white or clean isolated-background lighting for source assets. Shadows are soft, usually pale grey, and should anchor the characters lightly without creating heavy drama.

Rendering should include gentle surface texture: soft fabric on Patch’s hoodie and vest, mesh-like bib/vest texture on robot race tops, glossy screens, plastic/ceramic robot shells and subtle scuffs or seams on robot panels. Detail is acceptable when it clarifies material, not when it crowds the silhouette.

The general finish should be clean, warm and readable. Avoid high-contrast noir lighting, harsh metallic reflections, grimy battle damage or dense painterly backgrounds.

---

## 12. Composition Rules

For source assets, use full-body or near-full-body isolation. Leave enough margin around the character for later cropping. Keep the whole silhouette readable, especially antennae, wheels, shoes and bag straps.

Character art may include one or two supporting props, but the character must remain the focal point. Avoid busy backgrounds, crowd scenes or tournament environments in this asset pack unless the task specifically shifts from costume source art to scene art.

Recommended compositions:

- Single character centred on white background.
- Three-quarter view, slightly turned toward camera.
- Full-body pose with soft ground shadow.
- Prop held in hand or placed near feet when needed.

Avoid cropping off feet, wheels, antennae, bibs or essential accessories.

## 13. Do / Do Not

### Do

- Preserve Patch’s teal hood, antennae, cream vest, bib 042 and black terminal bag.
- Preserve Bit’s compact robot body, red accents, bib 007 and fast chaotic energy.
- Preserve Bot’s taller robot body, blue accents, bib 042, headband, wristbands and tied-on shoes.
- Keep bibs readable and character-specific.
- Use clean isolated backgrounds for reusable source assets.
- Keep silhouettes rounded, friendly and readable.

### Do Not

- Swap character colour systems.
- Remove Patch’s bag without reason.
- Remove Bot’s wheel-shoes gag in costume continuity scenes.
- Give Bit blue accents or Bot red accents.
- Turn Patch into a robot or the robots into humanlike androids.
- Use realistic photographic textures or gritty sports branding.
- Overload the characters with medals, banners or environment props from other asset packs unless explicitly needed.

## 15. Continuity Summary

This costume set is the canonical tournament-participant look for Patch, Bit and Bot in Issue #56. It should communicate that the trio are taking part in a structured, slightly absurd race of reasonable defaults. The humour comes from sincere participation: Patch takes notes carefully, Bit treats barriers as suggestions, and Bot earnestly wears shoes despite being a wheeled robot.

The strongest continuity signals are the bibs, colour-coded identities, athletic accessories and character-specific body language. Preserve those signals before adding scene-specific variation.
