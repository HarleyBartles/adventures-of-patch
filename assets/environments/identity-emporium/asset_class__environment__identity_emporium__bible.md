## Asset overview

The Identity Emporium is a literal costume-and-prop shop for mission identities.

It is not an abstract identity terminal, not a magical competence dispenser, not a fantasy RPG item shop, and not a premium heroic outfitter. It is a warm, grounded, slightly comic commercial store where agents could plausibly buy visible role surfaces and mission props before going to the real task domain.

The environment should read at a glance as:

A charming old-world costume shop stocked with role outfits, practical props, route-card-like stationery, paper tags, bags, receipts, fitting rooms, counters, and labelled inventory systems.

The shop has personality, but its personality comes from retail density, warm material texture, broad role-gear inventory, and practical shop logic. It should not need slogans or explanatory panels to communicate its function.

## Function and narrative role

In the story model, the Identity Emporium represents agent identity setup.

The shop supplies the visible surface of a role: costumes, props, tags, bags, receipts, forms, and role-coded gear. It does not itself supply competence, operating doctrine, domain grounding, success criteria, or task completion.

That narrative role creates the key visual rule:

The shop should look like it sells "role kits," not "identity completion."

The environment should therefore be practical and commercial. It can show racks, shelves, prop trays, checkout surfaces, fitting rooms, route-card stationery, and bagging areas. It should avoid visuals that imply transformation, enlightenment, automatic competence, magical upgrade, or abstract identity installation.

## Visual style

Use a warm illustrated Adventures visual style suitable for reusable environment asset sheets.

Core style:
- detailed but readable digital illustration;
- storybook/editorial warmth;
- clean shape language and clear object grouping;
- soft amber lighting;
- tactile shop textures;
- slightly comic commercial charm;
- practical retail density without clutter collapse.

The environment should feel browsable and usable. Even when the shop is packed with inventory, the image should preserve clear walking paths, shelf hierarchy, and location identity.

Avoid:
- photoreal retail render;
- glossy 3D game-shop look;
- fantasy concept-art armoury;
- high-tech sci-fi interface room;
- museum showroom grandeur;
- abstract infographic/poster design;
- dense omnibus wall of labels and panels.

## Extractable Prompt and QA Blocks

### Prompt-positive canon block

The Identity Emporium is a warm rustic practical costume-and-prop shop for mission identities. Show a literal commercial shop environment with deep forest green painted wood, aged timber shelves and floors, brass/gold trim, cream paper labels, warm amber lamps, old-fashioned counters, hooks, rails, cubbies, drawers, trunks, rugs, curtains, display tables, paper bags, receipts, tags, parcel string, and route-card-like stationery. The shop sells broad role costume and prop inventory for cowboy, detective, mechanic, chef, and future mission roles. The tone is charming, grounded, slightly comic, tactile, and browsable. Keep the environment dense but legible, with practical retail logic and clear location identity. This is an environment-only asset sheet.

### Prompt-negative canon block

Do not include Patch, Bit, Bot, the shopkeeper, customers, pedestrians, humans, living silhouettes, or character scale references. Do not make mannequins read as living characters. Do not depict a fantasy RPG shop, armoury, magic shop, heroic outfitter, museum showroom, abstract identity terminal, sci-fi office, high-tech setup lab, or magical competence dispenser. Do not include doctrine slogans, QA panels, process text, repo references, issue numbers, candidate labels, explanatory sidebars, or body-slide story panels. Do not combine all Stage 2b locations into one omnibus poster. Avoid dominant swords, shields, helmets, armour, glowing crystals, spell effects, holograms, weapon racks, or magical portals. Keep text minimal and in-world only.

### Sensitive detail micro-specs

Shop name:
Use "Identity Emporium" as the stable large shop name when a name appears. Do not rename it or substitute a generic costume-shop name.

Palette:
The shop family is anchored by deep forest green, warm aged wood, muted brass/gold, cream paper, amber light, muted burgundy textiles, leather/canvas neutrals, and dark brown/charcoal accents. Do not let blue UI glow, magical purple, chrome, sterile white, or steel armoury tones dominate.

Retail logic:
Objects should be arranged like shop inventory: racks, shelves, cubbies, labelled sections, display tables, counters, bags, receipts, tags, and fitting areas. Avoid abstract identity-system diagrams or symbolic process panels.

Role inventory:
Inventory should imply multiple role kits, especially cowboy, detective, mechanic, and chef. No one category should dominate the environment family unless the specific member sheet asks for it.

Route-card stationery:
Route-card-like forms may appear as paper objects, envelopes, clipboards, or counter stationery. They should not become large doctrine panels or readable lesson copy.

Text:
Use only minimal functional in-world labels. Generated text should be sparse and secondary. Avoid slogans and non-diegetic explanations.

Character contamination:
Environment-only sheets must contain no characters. Empty rooms, closed curtains, stocked shelves, and still-life retail detail are preferred.

### QA hard-gate checklist

A Stage 2b Identity Emporium environment candidate fails if any hard gate below fails:

1. Environment-only gate: The image contains no Patch, Bit, Bot, shopkeeper, customers, pedestrians, living silhouettes, or character scale references.
2. Location-sheet gate: The image focuses on one Stage 2b location or member environment, not an omnibus poster of all locations.
3. Identity gate: The environment reads as the Identity Emporium, a literal costume-and-prop shop, not a generic shop or unrelated environment.
4. Style-family gate: The image preserves the accepted green/wood/brass/cream/amber warm rustic shop family.
5. Practical retail gate: The space has usable commercial logic: shelves, racks, counters, tags, bags, receipts, fitting areas, or similar shop infrastructure.
6. Role-inventory gate: Costume and prop inventory suggests broad role gear rather than a single narrow category.
7. Anti-armoury gate: The image is not dominated by swords, shields, helmets, armour, weapons, magical artifacts, or RPG-item-shop cues.
8. Anti-terminal gate: The image is not an abstract identity terminal, high-tech setup lab, sci-fi UI space, portal room, or magical competence dispenser.
9. Text hygiene gate: The image contains no doctrine slogans, QA/process panels, repo references, issue numbers, candidate labels, or explanatory sidebars.
10. Reusability gate: The candidate is suitable as a reusable preproduction environment reference, not body-slide art or a narrative storyboard.

### Feature-specific repair blocks

Storefront exterior repair block:
Preserve the Identity Emporium's warm green/wood/brass/cream shop family. Repair the exterior so it reads as a charming independent costume-and-prop shop: green painted facade, gold "Identity Emporium" sign, cream-and-green striped awning, warm window displays, brass lamps, wood-and-glass door, and practical role-gear displays. Remove any characters, fantasy armoury cues, magical portal effects, or abstract terminal elements.

Main sales floor repair block:
Preserve the warm shop palette and costume-shop identity. Repair the main sales floor so it has clear retail browsing logic: central display tables, costume racks, shelves, drawers, hats, boots, bags, prop trays, rugs, lamps, and visible walking paths. Reduce clutter if objects collapse into an unreadable poster. Remove characters, process panels, and armoury dominance.

Costume and mixed prop wall repair block:
Preserve the environment-only lane and accepted shop family. Repair the wall into an organised role-gear display with sections for cowboy, detective, mechanic, chef, and future roles. Use shelves, hooks, rails, cubbies, small labels, boots below, hats above, and practical props in trays. Remove weapon-wall, RPG armoury, magic-shop, or slogan-poster cues.

Fitting room / fitting corridor repair block:
Preserve the warm retail material language. Repair the fitting area so it reads as an empty practical fitting corridor: curtains, mirror, stool, hooks, measuring tape, garment bags, rug runner, folded clothes, and lamps. Remove people, silhouettes behind curtains, transformation-chamber cues, glowing magic, or identity-machine elements.

Counter / checkout repair block:
Preserve the green/wood/brass/cream/amber palette. Repair the checkout area into a practical costume-shop counter: dark wood top, green panels, brass bell, banker lamp, receipt book, route-card forms, tags, string, paper bags, drawers, and small prop bins. Remove shopkeeper, Patch handoff staging, office terminal, doctrine poster, or explanatory process text.

Exit / bagging / receipt repair block:
Preserve the shop-family identity. Repair the exit beat into a warm transitional retail area with wrapped parcels, paper bags, stamped receipts, route-card envelopes, hooks, umbrella stand, boot mat, and warm door light. Remove character departure action, magic gateway, completed-mission proof, and "identity complete" slogan text.

Character contamination repair block:
Preserve the environment composition, palette, and useful shop details. Remove all characters, living silhouettes, pedestrians, customers, shopkeeper figures, Patch-like figures, Bit/Bot-like figures, and character scale references. Replace them with environment-only details such as empty racks, folded garments, paper bags, signs, lamps, counters, or still-life props.

Fantasy/RPG drift repair block:
Preserve the warm costume-shop setting and practical retail layout. Remove or de-emphasise swords, shields, helmets, armour, spellbooks, staffs, crystals, potions, monster trophies, and heroic weapon displays. Replace them with role costumes and ordinary practical props: hats, boots, coats, aprons, notebooks, magnifiers, tools, gloves, bags, route-card forms, and receipt stationery.

Operator-context repair block:
Preserve the visual shop environment. Remove all non-diegetic text, QA/process panels, issue references, repo words, candidate labels, sidebars, checklists, and lesson slogans. Use only sparse in-world shop labels such as "Fitting Room," "Checkout," "Props," "Hats," "Boots," "Cowboy," "Detective," "Mechanic," or "Chef."

Omnibus-poster repair block:
Preserve the accepted Identity Emporium style family. Repair the image into one focused location sheet rather than a combined poster of every shop area. Choose one member location as the dominant subject and allow only a few small in-world detail callouts if needed.

### Prompt guidance for image generation

For Stage 2b, build prompts in this order:

1. State the artifact lane: separate environment/location asset sheet.
2. State the specific member location: storefront exterior, main sales floor, prop wall, fitting corridor, checkout counter, or exit/bagging area.
3. Insert the prompt-positive canon block.
4. Add the location-specific clause.
5. Insert the prompt-negative canon block.
6. Add text hygiene and no-character constraints.
7. Keep any visible labels minimal and in-world.

Do not begin the prompt with story doctrine. Do not mention repo state, QA results, candidate history, skill names, session busters, or playbook mechanics. The prompt should describe only the visual subject.

## Stage 2b location-specific clauses

### Storefront exterior clause

Focus on the exterior storefront of the Identity Emporium: deep green painted facade, gold-lettered "Identity Emporium" sign, cream-and-green striped awning, brass lamps, warm window displays of hats, coats, boots, bags, props, and route-card stationery, wood-and-glass door, cosy independent shop presence, and practical commercial charm. ### Main sales floor clause

Focus on the main sales floor: central display tables, costume racks, role-gear shelves, hat and boot displays, prop drawers, warm rugs, amber lamps, clear walking paths, dense but legible inventory, and broad role gear for cowboy, detective, mechanic, chef, and future missions. No characters.

### Costume and mixed prop wall clause

Focus on one organised role-gear wall: labelled sections for cowboy, detective, mechanic, chef, and future roles; shelves and hooks for hats, coats, boots, bags, notebooks, gloves, aprons, goggles, tools, rope, magnifiers, and practical props. The wall should read as a costume-and-prop display, not a weapon rack.

### Fitting room / fitting corridor clause

Focus on the fitting area: warm narrow corridor, closed curtains, mirrors, stools, hooks, measuring tape, garment bags, rug runner, folded clothing, soft lamps, and empty practical try-on space. No people, no silhouettes, no transformation device.

### Counter / checkout clause

Focus on the checkout counter: dark wood top, green painted panels, brass bell, banker lamp, receipt book, clipboard, route-card forms, paper bags, tags, string, drawers, small prop bins, and practical retail packing details. No shopkeeper and no handoff scene.

### Exit / bagging / receipt clause

Focus on the exit and bagging area: wrapped parcels, paper bags, stamped receipts, route-card envelopes, hooks, umbrella stand, boot mat, warm front-door light, and ready-to-leave retail beat. The area should imply "gear is packed for departure" without showing any character leaving.
