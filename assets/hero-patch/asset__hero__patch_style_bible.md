Patch Character Visual Design Guide v1.4

Patch is often shown navigating tasks and processes, following structured rules and cooperating with others to complete his work.

Version note: v1.4 keeps Patch canon unchanged and adds extractable prompt, QA, repair and extrapolation blocks for generation and QA workflows. v1.3 hardened Patch's eye canon. Patch's eyes remain identity-critical: simple black oval eyes with small white shine highlights only. Do not invent alternate eye styles.

Character Overview

Patch is stylised as a friendly autonomous software agent with a clean, modern look. He is drawn in an editorial vector style with thick, rounded outlines and soft shading. His body is compact and proportional, standing roughly mid-sized compared to an average human. Patch's head is round with a simple expressive face; two short antennae protrude from his hoodie, hinting at his digital nature.

Approval, warning or rejection colours should not compete with Patch's teal identity, and they should not alter his hoodie, bag, pants, shoes, face, antennae or core silhouette.

Clothing and Accessories

Hoodie: Patch always wears a teal hoodie with a front pouch pocket and two antennae protruding from the hood. The hood is usually up, but may be down when Patch is at rest.

Crossbody bag: Patch carries a teal crossbody bag with a small greater-than symbol (`>`) on the flap. This bag holds his payloads, receipts, plans, records and rows. The bag strap always crosses his torso from shoulder to opposite hip; the bag is never worn over one shoulder like a tote or shoulder bag. The bag normally rests at Patch's right hip, but it may shift to his left hip when a composition needs to keep his body readable. In a continuous scene, immediate sequence, mirrored setup, or before/after comparison, the bag must not switch shoulder/hip sides unless the continuity of the scene clearly explains that movement. The bag should never appear both in front and behind Patch at the same time, including in mirror reflections. In reflected views, show the same single crossbody bag consistently, respecting the mirror orientation without duplicating it.

Pants and shoes: Dark pants and teal shoes with white soles anchor Patch's outfit. The shoes have rounded toes and simple detailing.

Eyes and Face

Patch's eyes are identity-critical.

Canonical Patch eyes:

- simple black oval eyes;
- small white shine highlights only;
- no white sclera or white eye fields;
- no visible iris or coloured pupil;
- no anime, human, animal, doll, toy, or generic mascot eye construction;
- no large expressive cartoon eyes that change the eye anatomy;
- no eyelid-heavy expressions that imply human eyes;
- no creative alternate eye styles.

The white mark in Patch's eye is a shine highlight, not a pupil and not a white eye area. Keep it small and consistent.

Patch's face is simple but expressive. Expression should come mainly from posture, head angle, mouth curve, body language, and simple brow/face marks when appropriate. Do not alter Patch's eye construction to create emotion.

Expression guidance:

- Curiosity: open posture, slight head tilt, attentive black oval eyes with small shine highlights.
- Determination: forward lean, focused body language, simple brow/face emphasis without changing eye anatomy.
- Uncertainty: small flat or uncertain mouth, cautious pose.
- Relief: gentle smile, relaxed pose.

Bag Placement and Continuity

Patch's bag has specific semantic rules because it is part of how audiences recognise him and read the scene:

- The bag is always a crossbody bag, never a one-shoulder bag.
- The strap must visibly cross Patch's torso from one shoulder to the opposite hip whenever his torso is visible.
- The bag usually rests at Patch's right hip.
- The bag may rest at Patch's left hip when composition requires it, especially to avoid blocking important body language or a foreground object.
- The bag must not switch sides within a sequence unless the scene clearly shows or implies that Patch has repositioned it.
- The bag must never appear as both a front bag and a back bag in the same frame.
- Mirror reflections must preserve the same single bag. The reflection may reverse orientation, but it must not create a second bag or imply that Patch is wearing two bags.
- When a frame is intended to be a comparison, a direct continuation, or the same moment shown in different contexts, keep the bag placement consistent between panels.

Expressions and Poses

Patch communicates through simple face marks, mouth curvature, posture and gesture while preserving canonical eye construction. Poses should feel natural and active: walking; pondering with a hand on his chin; checking items in his bag; or presenting documents or data to another person or system. Keep limbs proportionate and slightly rounded to match the overall aesthetic.

Scenes and Environments

Patch appears in various settings. Use these environment guidelines:

Interior spaces: Dim or warm interior settings, such as quiet workspaces or meeting rooms. Contrast Patch with lighter surfaces to keep him visible.

Exterior spaces: Urban or generic outdoor scenes with signage or access points. Light sources may reflect on Patch's hoodie and bag.

Preparation scenes: Bright indoor environments with neutral walls, off-white or light grey. Patch organises his bag or checks his reflection in a mirror before heading out.

Presentation scenes: Simple backgrounds that do not distract from Patch. Use subtle gradients or abstract shapes in light tones.

Do's and Don'ts

Do: Keep Patch's hoodie teal and his bag present in all action scenes.

Do: Maintain rounded, thick outlines and minimal shading.

Do: Use the defined colour palette when adding new props or accessories.

Do: Use black oval eyes with small white shine highlights only.

Don't: Depict Patch as aggressive or villainous.

Don't: Add complex patterns or logos that distract from the design.

Don't: Change the core proportions or facial features.

Don't: give Patch white sclera, visible irises, coloured pupils, anime eyes, human eyes, doll eyes, animal eyes, or alternate creative eye styles.

Extractable Prompt and QA Blocks

Use the blocks below when building image-generation prompts, failed-QA repair prompts, or Patch-bearing QA hard gates. Quote or adapt only the blocks relevant to the current image lane. Preserve exact canon where it matters. Do not paste QA prose, issue comments, candidate numbers, or process notes into image prompts.

Prompt-positive canon block

Canonical Patch is a friendly autonomous software-agent character drawn in a clean editorial vector style with thick rounded outlines and soft shading. Patch is compact and proportional. He wears a long-sleeved teal hoodie with the hood up, a front pouch pocket, and two slim dark antenna stalks protruding from the hood, each ending in a small dark rounded tip. The teal hood frames an off-white face panel. Patch's eyes are simple black oval eyes with tiny white shine highlights only. Patch wears dark trousers and teal shoes with white soles. Patch carries a distinct teal crossbody bag with a dark strap crossing the torso from shoulder to opposite hip; the bag has a dark or black flap/background with a white greater-than symbol (`>`) on it. Patch should read as the same hooded character shown in the approved contact sheet, not as a generic robot wearing teal.

Prompt-negative canon block

Do not depict Patch as a generic robot, cute robot, toy, mascot, plush, human boy, anime/chibi character, glossy 3D character, photoreal character, or screen-face bot. Do not give Patch a black screen face, visor face, glowing display face, white sclera, irises, coloured pupils, large cartoon eyes, doll eyes, animal eyes, or alternate eye styles. Do not expose segmented robot arms or legs as the main body read. Do not replace Patch's long-sleeved teal hoodie, dark trousers, teal shoes, hood-framed off-white face panel, antennae, or crossbody bag. Do not use a `P` logo, name tag, badge, or other substitute branding as Patch identity. Do not make other agents into Patch clones or give them Patch's hoodie, antennae, bag, face panel, full silhouette, or `>` symbol.

Sensitive detail micro-specs

Eyes: Patch's eyes are simple black oval eyes on the off-white face panel, each with only a tiny white shine highlight. The white mark is a shine highlight, not a pupil, sclera, or white eye field.

Antennae: Patch has two slim dark antenna stalks protruding from the teal hood. Each stalk ends in a small dark rounded tip. They are modest in scale, usually symmetrical, slightly angled outward, and integrated into the hood silhouette. They are not thick, teal, oversized, droopy, alien-like, toy-like, bobble-dominant, or separate robot hardware.

Bag: Patch's bag is a crossbody bag, visually distinct from the hoodie. The strap is dark and visibly crosses the torso from shoulder to opposite hip when the torso is visible. The bag usually rests at Patch's right hip. The visible flap/logo area should be dark or black with a white `>` mark. The bag must not blend into the hoodie as an all-teal blob and must not become a one-shoulder tote.

Hoodie and body: Patch's hoodie is a long-sleeved teal hoodie with the hood up in most active scenes. The hoodie frames the off-white face panel. Patch should not read as a separate robot body wearing a sleeveless hoodie shell. Do not expose white segmented robot limbs as the main arm or leg construction.

Trousers and shoes: Patch wears dark trousers and teal shoes with white soles. Do not substitute white robot legs, robot boots, bare mechanical feet, or generic mascot shoes.

Face and hood relationship: The off-white face panel belongs inside the teal hood shape. Do not draw a standalone robot head with a hoodie added around it. Avoid black face screens, visors, glowing panels, or flat display heads.

Non-robot construction: Patch may hint at digital nature through antennae and stylisation, but the visual read is a hooded autonomous-agent character. Prompts should not call him a robot, cute robot, toy robot, mascot, or bot.

QA hard-gate checklist

For any Patch-bearing candidate, check these gates before evaluating scene quality:

1. Whole character: Does the figure read as canonical Patch from the contact sheet, not a generic robot or mascot?
2. Hoodie and body: Is the long-sleeved teal hoodie the main body read, with dark trousers and teal shoes with white soles?
3. Face and eyes: Is the off-white face panel framed by the hood, with simple black oval eyes and tiny white shine highlights only?
4. Antennae: Are the antennae slim, dark, modest, hood-integrated stalks with small dark rounded tips?
5. Bag: Is the crossbody bag distinct from the hoodie, with a dark strap and a white `>` mark on a dark/black flap or background?
6. Non-robot drift: Are there no exposed segmented robot limbs, screen face, visor, toy-bot body, or substitute identity marks?
7. Singularity and distinction: Is there one Patch, and are all other people, agents, systems, or bots clearly non-Patch?
8. Style fit: Does the image stay in the approved editorial-vector style rather than CGI, photoreal, plush, anime, or over-detailed concept art?

If any hard gate fails, the candidate is not accepted as Patch-bearing art or Patch-bearing preproduction reference, even if the surrounding scene is attractive.

Feature-specific repair blocks

Use these repair blocks when a candidate fails a specific Patch feature. Preserve any correct features from the previous candidate explicitly.

Antenna repair block: Preserve Patch's correct hoodie, face panel, eyes, trousers, shoes, bag and pose. Repair only the antennae: draw two slim dark stalks protruding from the teal hood, each ending in a small dark rounded tip, modest in scale, slightly angled outward, and integrated into the hood silhouette. Do not draw thick, teal, oversized, alien, toy, drooping, bobble-dominant, or separate robot antennae.

Bag repair block: Preserve Patch's correct hoodie, face, eyes, antennae, trousers, shoes and pose. Repair only the bag: make it a distinct teal crossbody bag with a dark strap crossing the torso from shoulder to opposite hip. Show a dark or black flap/background with a white `>` mark. Do not let the bag blend into the hoodie, become all teal, switch to a one-shoulder bag, or duplicate across front/back/mirror views.

Eye repair block: Preserve Patch's correct hoodie, antennae, body, trousers, shoes, bag and pose. Repair only the eyes and face panel: draw simple black oval eyes on the off-white face panel with tiny white shine highlights only. Do not draw white sclera, irises, pupils, coloured eyes, glowing display eyes, anime eyes, human eyes, doll eyes, animal eyes, or large mascot eyes.

Robot-body repair block: Preserve any correct scene, pose, hoodie colour, face panel, eyes, antennae and bag. Repair Patch's body read: Patch must wear a long-sleeved teal hoodie as the primary body garment, dark trousers, and teal shoes with white soles. Do not draw exposed white segmented robot arms or legs, robot feet, mechanical joints, or a separate robot body wearing a hoodie shell.

Face-screen repair block: Preserve any correct hoodie, antennae, trousers, shoes, bag and pose. Repair Patch's head/face architecture: the teal hood frames an off-white face panel with simple black oval eyes. Do not draw a black screen face, visor, glowing display face, robot display head, or standalone robot head with clothing added around it.

Patch-clone repair block: Preserve Patch as the only Patch. Make other characters, agents, bots, humans, systems or support roles visually distinct from Patch. Do not give them Patch's teal hoodie, hood antennae, crossbody bag, `>` mark, off-white face panel, or full silhouette.

Extrapolation rules

Safe to vary by scene:

- Patch's pose, gesture, mouth curve, brow/face emphasis and body language;
- bag hip side when composition requires it, if continuity remains clear;
- lighting, shadows and scene-specific prop accents;
- small held props, documents, route cards, tools or costume pieces, as long as they do not obscure core Patch identity;
- costumes or role gear added over/with Patch when the story requires it, as long as Patch remains recognisable and the hoodie/body/bag/face/antennae identity is not overwritten.

Do not vary without explicit canon update:

- eye anatomy;
- face-panel architecture;
- antenna geometry family;
- long-sleeved teal hoodie as Patch's main identity garment;
- dark trousers and teal shoes with white soles;
- crossbody bag semantics and visible strap when torso is visible;
- `>` bag mark as Patch's bag mark;
- Patch singularity and non-clone rule.

Prompt Guidance for Image Generation

When using an image generator to create new illustrations featuring Patch, start with the prompt-positive canon block and then add lane, scene and prop detail. Do not start with the scene and then add Patch as a robot character inside it. For active visual-preproduction loops, also include the relevant negative canon block, text budget and operator-context quarantine.

Describe Patch clearly: use whole-character language from the prompt-positive canon block. Specify that the bag is worn crossbody, normally at his right hip, and that continuity must be preserved across panels or reflections.

Specify the style: request a clean, vector editorial style with thick outlines and soft shading.

Set the scene context only after Patch identity is locked: e.g. "preparing indoors," "entering a work area," "sharing documents or data with a colleague," or another deck-specific frame.

Avoid real brands or artists: keep prompts generic and original.

Example prompt:

Create a clean, vector-style illustration of a friendly autonomous software-agent character named Patch. Patch wears a long-sleeved teal hoodie with the hood up, a front pouch pocket, and two slim dark antenna stalks protruding from the hood, each ending in a small dark rounded tip. The teal hood frames an off-white face panel. Patch has simple black oval eyes with tiny white shine highlights only, no white sclera and no irises. Patch wears dark trousers and teal shoes with white soles. He carries a distinct teal crossbody bag with a dark strap crossing the torso from shoulder to opposite hip and a dark flap with a white `>` mark. Place Patch in a collaborative workspace, politely sharing documents or data with a larger colleague. Use thick rounded outlines, simple soft shading and a clean editorial-vector style. Do not draw Patch as a generic robot, screen-face bot, toy mascot, plush character, photoreal character, anime character, or 3D render.
