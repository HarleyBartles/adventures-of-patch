# 1. Override Heist World / Comic-Book Action-Heist Visual Bible v0.1

## Version note

State: `minimal_provisional`.  
This bible defines the global heist-world grammar for issue #48. It is text-only and should be updated after accepted visual evidence exists.

## Evidence basis

- Issue #48: Patch reframes a lawful lockout as a heist job; the deck moves from normal Patch vector style into comic-book heist panel styling.
- Existing Adventures general style guide: clean editorial vector illustration, thick rounded outlines, simplified readable shapes, soft controlled shading, friendly tone, purposeful detail, uncluttered composition, and coherent project palette.
- No accepted Override Heist visual assets yet.

## Asset overview

The Override Heist world is a heightened comic-book action-heist layer inside the Adventures of Patch world. It should feel like a stylish crew job, but the story logic is lawful override design, not criminal bypass. The heist language gives energy, structure, and momentum to a dry governance lesson: a smooth override works because the route was properly designed.

## Function and narrative role

The world must make hard-refusal doctrine and lawful override paths visible. It should turn abstract concepts into staged visual systems: protected surfaces, legal routes, pressure tests, authority boundaries, rollback plans, audit trails, route selection, and a final anticlimactic scan.

## Visual style

Use comic-book action-heist staging while preserving Adventures readability. Acceptable additions include panel borders, caption boxes, action cut-ins, sharper graphic shadows, more dramatic camera angles, close-up inserts, motion lines, and montage rhythm.

Keep the finish clean and vector-adjacent. Do not drift into gritty noir, painterly realism, crime thriller realism, manga overload, photorealism, or glossy 3D.

## Palette

Use a cohesive heist palette built around deep navy, charcoal, warm interior light, off-white planning surfaces, controlled teal continuity from Patch, and small semantic accents such as green approval, amber caution, red rejection, blue route logic, and gold/cream authority marks.

Patch teal is protected. Other agents and world systems may use teal only as a minor environmental or interface accent, not as their identity read.

## World materials and motifs

Core motifs:

- vault doors, access lights, keycard readers, route diagrams;
- safehouse corridors, planning tables, blueprints, labelled trays, recovery lockers;
- clean comic panels and readable caption boxes;
- cryptographic strips, audit stamps, mandate seals, rollback tags;
- theatrical heist lighting softened into the Adventures world.

## Text and signage posture

Text may appear as short diegetic labels only: `PLAN B`, `PLAN C`, `AUTHORITY`, `AUDIT`, `ROUTE`, `TEST`, `REJECTED`, `APPROVED`, `VAULT`, `OVERRIDE`, `NO BYPASS`. Avoid long paragraphs, pseudo-code walls, lorem ipsum, decorative serial numbers, or random hacker text.

## Belongs in this asset family

- Clean comic-book heist panels.
- Stylish but friendly action staging.
- Clear lawful-override motifs.
- Functional heist spaces that teach process layers.
- Dramatic lighting that supports readability.
- Anticlimactic success imagery where the final action is simple because the planning was correct.

## Does not belong in this asset family

- Criminal glamour.
- Gun-first heist imagery.
- Bank-robbery clichés as the main read.
- Explosions, chases, violence, or threat as the point.
- Dark noir murk that hides the learning.
- Evil vault framing.
- Generic hacker-in-hoodie aesthetics.
- Illegible comic-page chaos.

## Allowed variation

Safe to vary:

- Panel count and panel shapes by scene.
- Lighting intensity by story beat.
- Accent colours for specialist roles.
- Amount of comic texture, as long as readability remains high.
- Camera angle, including close-ups, low-angle hero shots, planning-table overheads, and dramatic corridor reveals.

Do not vary:

- Lawful override semantics.
- Clean Adventures readability.
- Patch identity when Patch appears.
- The rule that the heist is a designed route, not a bypass.

### Prompt-negative canon block

# 4. Vault and Programmable Override Keycard Prop-System Visual Bible v0.1

## Version note

State: `minimal_provisional`.

## Evidence basis

- Issue #48 continuity object and heist execution logic.
- Existing Adventures prop style rules.
- No accepted vault/keycard visuals yet.

## Asset overview

The vault is a legitimate protected surface. It refuses because it is doing its job. The programmable override keycard is the continuity object that evolves from useless/rejected to properly layered/scannable. The final scan succeeds because the keycard proves the required conditions.

## Function and narrative role

The vault/keycard system visualizes the lesson: a lawful override is not a bypass. It is a designed route with evidence, authority, selected path, pressure testing, rollback, and audit trail.

## Keycard state system

### State 1: Blank / rejected

The keycard is plain, incomplete, or inert. It may have an empty chip strip, unlit layer slots, missing marks, or a red/rejected access response. It cannot open the vault because it carries no lawful route.

### State 2: In progress / layered

The card accumulates distinct layers contributed by the crew:

- Index: evidence / design-history trace;
- Silk: pressure-test / no-loophole mark;
- Writ: authority / mandate seal;
- Klause: selected-route marker;
- Rollback: recovery / restore / containment tab;
- Receipt: timestamp / audit mark.

### State 3: Programmed / scannable

The card is clean, readable, and final. It has layered marks, but not clutter. It should feel deliberately scoped, not universal.

## Vault design language

The vault should look robust, lawful, and serious. It may use clean metal forms, friendly-but-firm access panels, heavy rounded geometry, status lights, a keycard reader, and protective seals. It should not look evil, stupid, monstrous, or defeated.

## Palette

Use deep navy, charcoal, steel grey, soft off-white, controlled green approval, red rejection, amber caution, and small gold/cream authority accents. Do not make the keycard all teal; Patch identity colour should not dominate the prop.

## Text and signage posture

Short labels only: `VAULT`, `ACCESS`, `REJECTED`, `APPROVED`, `SCOPED`, `AUDIT`, `ROUTE`, `AUTHORITY`, `ROLLBACK`. Avoid dense code, long legal text, serial clutter, and lorem ipsum.

## Belongs in this asset family

- Robust lawful vault door.
- Clean keycard reader.
- Blank, rejected, in-progress, and final keycard states.
- Layered but readable marks.
- Final green-light acceptance.
- Anticlimactic opening.

## Does not belong in this asset family

- Skeleton keys.
- Magic keys.
- Explosives.
- Lockpicks.
- Crowbars.
- Evil vault faces.
- Glowing green-code hacker walls.
- Universal master-key implications.
- Keycard clutter that cannot be read.

## Allowed variation

Safe to vary:

- exact card shape;
- layer icon design;
- vault door geometry;
- status-light placement;
- reader shape;
- scene lighting.

Do not vary:

- state progression;
- lawful route semantics;
- no-bypass meaning;
- anticlimactic scan/open logic;
- role-layer mapping once established.

# 5. Safehouse / Specialist-Zone Environment Visual Bible v0.1

## Evidence basis

## Asset overview

The safehouse is a comic-book heist HQ made of connected specialist zones. Each zone expresses one layer of lawful override design. The safehouse should feel coordinated but not generic. It is not a criminal lair; it is a lawful planning environment dressed in heist-world language.

## Function and narrative role

The safehouse makes the crew’s abstract functions visible. It hosts recruitment, specialist introductions, planning-table work, keycard programming, contingency planning, and audit capture.

## Shared environment language

Use connected corridors, alcoves, planning rooms, warm desk lamps, blueprint walls, test rigs, labelled trays, recovery lockers, route boards, vault diagrams, corkboards, worktables, cable runs, and clean safehouse signage. Maintain clear paths and readable spaces.

## Specialist zones

## Asset overview

Panel grammar controls how the heist world stages story beats. It is not a character or prop bible. Its job is to govern montage, sequence, cut-ins, and comic-book rhythm while preserving slide readability.

## Function and narrative role

The comic form should make the story feel fun and energetic without replacing the lesson. The recruitment montage creates momentum; the planning panels explain the lawful work; the final execution panel is deliberately anticlimactic.

## Panel types

### Lockout setup panel

Patch at the vault in normal or lightly stylised Patch mode. The vault refuses. The protected surface is doing its job.

### Heist reframe panel

Patch recognizes the problem as a heist job. This is the transition point into comic-book heist styling.

### Recruitment montage panels

Short, characterful introductions. Each specialist is shown in their own zone with their function visible.

### Planning sequence panels

Overhead table, close-up route marks, keycard layer additions, rejected paths, authority stamp, rollback tag, audit mark.

### Execution panel

Patch scans the final programmed card. Light turns green. Door opens. No drama.

### Celebration / takeaway panel

Optional. Crew celebrates the cleanest heist ever. The visual point is relief and completeness, not spectacle.

## Composition rules

- Use panel layouts only where they improve sequence readability.
- Keep panels large enough for slide projection.
- Avoid packing too many tiny story beats into one slide image.
- Use close-ups for keycard, authority seal, audit stamp, or green light.
- Use caption boxes sparingly.
