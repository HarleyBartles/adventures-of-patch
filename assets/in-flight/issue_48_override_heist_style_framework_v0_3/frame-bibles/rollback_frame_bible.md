# Rollback Frame Bible

Version: v0.3  
State: `minimal_provisional_frame_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit frame bible

## Function

Rollback's frame grammar makes recovery, containment, abort conditions, and Plan B logic visible through an active observation-deck route.

## Frame domain

Rollback's panels are chunky, load-bearing observation-window panels. They feel like reinforced bunker architecture: heavy glass, steel shutters, control panels, thick borders, containment doors, hazard margins, and stable infrastructure.

The slide is a linear inspection route along the observation deck. The room progression is the recruitment slide progression.

## Motion logic

The frame sequence should show controlled preparedness:

1. Plan A is catastrophically failing behind glass.
2. Rollback calmly locks it down and a shutter snaps over the observation window.
3. Other rooms show controlled backup simulations.
4. Room labels escalate from simple plans to elaborate failure-on-failure scenarios.
5. The end state proves that a plan without recovery is not a plan.

## Positive constraint block

```text
Use Rollback's containment observation-deck frame grammar. Panels are chunky reinforced observation windows, heavy glass, steel shutters, control panels, thick borders, containment doors, hazard margins, and stable infrastructure. The slide moves linearly along an active contingency bunker observation deck. Plan A is visibly failing and gets calmly sealed; later windows show stable controlled backup scenarios and escalating Plan B / Plan C / failure-on-failure rooms.
```

## Negative constraint block

```text
Do not make Rollback's frame grammar a weapons armoury, combat bunker, war room, or generic warehouse. Do not make every panel chaotic; only Plan A should be catastrophic. Do not make the observation windows decorative; they are the structured recovery/containment progression.
```

## QA gates

- Does the room/window progression carry the recruitment slide?
- Is Plan A the only catastrophic failure?
- Are backup simulations controlled and increasingly imaginative?
- Do the heavy borders, glass, and shutters support recovery/containment rather than combat fantasy?
- Is the frame grammar clearly distinct from Writ's institutional panels and Klause's grid?
