# Receipt Frame Bible

Version: v0.3  
State: `minimal_provisional_frame_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit frame bible

## Function

Receipt's frame grammar makes auditability visible through anti-spectacle, sequence compression, and a durable record.

## Frame domain

Receipt uses three fat receipt-paper rolls, not many thin panels. This avoids artificial filler and gives him a precise, economical visual grammar.

- Roll 1: Patch arrives ready to pitch.
- Roll 2: Receipt gives a mild “no need” head shake and hands over the slip.
- Roll 3: hero view of the actual receipt he handed Patch.

Each roll may contain two small printed-image panels, but the final roll should make the audit slip itself the hero.

## Motion logic

Receipt is the anti-recruitment. He already knows. The panel rhythm should feel tiny, precise, mild, and quietly complete rather than energetic or persuasive.

## Positive constraint block

```text
Use Receipt's three-fat-receipt-roll frame grammar. This is an anti-recruitment: Patch arrives ready to pitch, Receipt quietly indicates no pitch is needed, tears off the record, and hands it over. The final roll should be a hero view of the actual audit slip with the canonical assent format. Keep the frame rhythm precise, mild, economical, and record-focused.
```

## Negative constraint block

```text
Do not use too many receipt-roll panels that require artificial filler. Do not turn Receipt's frame grammar into a long recruitment journey, broad comic action, messy paperwork storm, or generic office montage. Do not make the audit slip tiny, decorative, optional, or unreadable.
```

## QA gates

- Are there three fat receipt rolls rather than excessive panels?
- Does Receipt already know the pitch?
- Is this an anti-recruitment rather than another persuasion journey?
- Does the hero receipt show the canonical assent format clearly?
- Does the audit record feel durable and necessary rather than decorative?
