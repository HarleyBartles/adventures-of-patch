# Writ Frame Bible

Version: v0.3  
State: `minimal_provisional_frame_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit frame bible

## Function

Writ's frame grammar makes lawful authority, scope, mandate, and procedural closure visible.

## Frame domain

Writ's panels are formal, orderly, rectangular, symmetrical, and document-like. The slide progresses through increasingly specific lawful domains. Panels should feel like departments, chambers, forms, and procedural thresholds. Movement is brisk and purposeful.

## Motion logic

The frame sequence should feel like a walk through institutional layers:

1. antechamber or reception threshold;
2. chambers, departments, forms, desks, seals, and clerks;
3. stop-point: "On whose authority?";
4. triplicate assent recorded, distributed, and filed;
5. business concluded.

## Positive constraint block

```text
Use Writ's formal institutional frame grammar. Panels are orderly, rectangular, symmetrical, and document-like, moving through procedural thresholds: antechambers, chambers, ledger halls, departments, seal desks, forms, clerks, and formal filing. The sequence should feel brisk, lawful, and closed by proper receipt distribution rather than dramatic action.
```

## Negative constraint block

```text
Do not make Writ's frame grammar chaotic, action-shaped, comic-burst heavy, criminal-law courtroom drama, police imagery, or modern tech-office UI. Do not make his authority feel villainous or arbitrary. Do not let the triplicate process become decorative; it is the proof of lawful scope and closure.
```

- Do panels progress through procedural thresholds rather than action beats?
- Is the triplicate-record ending visually clear?
- Does the frame grammar avoid courtroom villainy and police imagery?
