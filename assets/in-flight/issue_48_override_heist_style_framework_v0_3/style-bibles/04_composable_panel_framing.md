## Purpose

This bible defines shared panel, frame, gutter, and superframe doctrine for the Override Heist. It sits above the character-specific frame bibles and below the global world bible. Its core principle: **panel style is not decoration; panel style is a character's mode of reasoning made visible.**

## Ownership rule

This file owns global frame taxonomy and deck-safe panel rules.

Character-specific frame behaviour belongs in `frame-bibles/`. Character identity belongs in `character-bibles/`. Do not overload character bibles with frame-transition catalogues or panel-grammar taxonomies.

## Shared taxonomy

- **Panel:** a bounded visual unit. In Override Heist, panels may be rooms, documents, receipts, observation windows, decision states, or process beats.
- **Frame:** the visible boundary around a panel. It may be graphic only, but in some character grammars it becomes a physical object.
- **Border:** the stroke/lip/material edge of a frame. Silk treats this as manipulable architecture; other recruits usually treat it as document, window, institutional margin, grid line, or receipt edge.
- **Gutter:** the space between panels. It may be empty reading space, a crawlable seam, a procedural threshold, or a conceptual jump depending on the frame bible.
- **Superframe:** the larger page/slide structure that holds multiple panels and governs the route or sequence across them.
- **Inset:** a smaller panel or cut-in that clarifies evidence, tool use, status, or consequence without taking over the main route.
- **Caption:** short editorial text. Use sparingly. It may clarify a payoff, but should not do the job of composition.
- **Diegetic label:** text that exists inside the scene, such as `TEST`, `REJECTED`, `PLAN B`, `AUDIT`, or `I'M IN`.
- **Route line:** a visible line, string, path, highlight, or object continuity that guides traversal or logic. It must mean something; it is not decoration.
- **Physical panel-domain:** panels behave like places or objects the characters can occupy, cross, open, close, inspect, or pass behind.
- **Process panel-domain:** panels represent internal logic, procedural steps, audit order, decision state, or layer assembly.

## General panel rule

Panels should be readable at slide scale and should support cause-and-effect. Use comic-book grammar to expose reasoning and process, not to add noise.

## No-floating-arrow doctrine

Avoid floating arrows laid over panels. If attention or sequence needs direction, use panel geometry, route-line continuity inside the scene, lighting, object motion, character movement, or a shaped gutter/panel edge. If a panel sequence needs a pointer, shape the panel itself as the pointer.

## Panels as physical or process domain

For most recruits, panel layout is not merely page design. It is the recruit's reasoning domain made visible.

- Index's panels are old documents and the paper trail Patch walks through.
- Silk's panels are the test corridor route Patch and Silk physically traverse.
- Rollback's panels are observation-window rooms along a containment inspection deck.
- Writ's panels are institutional lawful-authority departments and chambers.
- Klause's panels are internal decision-state grid beats, not a room traversal.
- Receipt's panels are audit receipt rolls and the durable record itself.

Physical-domain panels need legible traversal, thresholds, or interaction. Process-domain panels need legible sequence, rejection, selection, or record logic.

## Default deck grammar

Use a clear classic comic rhythm as the default deck-safe panel language: restrained rectangular panels, clean sequence, good cause-and-effect, controlled variation.

Use energetic action-shaped panels selectively: angled heist montage, route-shaped panels, scan-beam panels, circular status insets, and burst panels should be reserved for strong process beats or signature slides.

## Character-specific frame grammar routing

Use `frame-bibles/00_frame_bible_index.md` to choose the relevant frame bible.

- **Index:** archive document panels; dog-eared, folded, dusty, overlapping, clipped, with reveal-window occlusion.
- **Silk:** thin, tense route-testing panels; red string, dead ends, switchbacks, narrow crawl spaces, rappel drops, boundary crossings.
- **Rollback:** chunky observation-deck panels; containment rooms, glass windows, shutters, big load-bearing borders.
- **Writ:** formal institutional panels; orderly rectangles, brass margins, office/chamber thresholds, triplicate-document logic.
- **Klause:** clean nine-panel internal decision grid; posture and panel viability show mental rejection.
- **Receipt:** three fat receipt-roll columns; minimal anti-recruitment, audit slip as hero.

## Planning-table reuse rule

The planning-table slide should reuse the six recruitment grammars as small inserts around the keycard. Each insert should read as a layer contributed by a recruit: paper-trail/provenance, stress-tested route, contingency recovery, authority mandate, selected decision, audit record.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Use composable Override Heist frame grammar where panel style expresses reasoning. Keep panels readable at slide scale with clean Adventures editorial-vector finish. Use panel geometry, gutters, route continuity, lighting, object motion, and character movement to direct attention rather than floating arrows. Let each recruit's frame language reflect their domain: archive documents for Index, tensile test route for Silk, containment observation deck for Rollback, orderly lawful chambers for Writ, nine-panel internal decision grid for Klause, and receipt-roll audit strips for Receipt. Use dynamic panel shapes only where the shape itself tells the process.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not use chaotic unreadable comic pages, tiny overloaded panels, floating arrows over panels, dense dialogue, manga clutter, random sound effects, decorative panel shapes with no story function, or panel drama that hides the lesson. Do not make every recruit use the same panel rhythm. Do not use dynamic action panels as the default when calm editorial panels would explain the beat better. Do not put a character-specific transition catalogue into a character bible when it belongs in a frame bible.
```

## QA hard gates

- Does the panel style communicate process rather than decoration?
- Are panels readable at slide scale?
- Is the recruit's reasoning mode visible in the panel grammar?
- Are arrows avoided or integrated into panel shape, scene route, lighting, or object motion?
- Is the sequence understandable without too much text?
- Does panel energy support the lawful-override lesson rather than overwhelm it?
- If a panel crossing happens, is the crossing visible rather than implied by a jump cut?
- If the panel grammar is character-specific, is the matching frame bible being used?
