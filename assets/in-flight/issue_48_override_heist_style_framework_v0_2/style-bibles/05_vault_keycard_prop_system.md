## Asset overview

The vault is a legitimate protected surface. It refuses because it is doing its job. The programmable override keycard is the continuity object that evolves from useless/rejected to properly layered/scannable. The final scan succeeds because the keycard proves the required conditions.

## Function and narrative role

The vault/keycard system visualizes the lesson: a lawful override is not a bypass. It is a designed route with evidence, authority, selected path, pressure testing, rollback, and audit trail.

## Keycard state system

### State 1: Blank / rejected

The keycard is plain, incomplete, or inert. It may have an empty chip strip, unlit layer slots, missing marks, or a red/rejected access response. It cannot open the vault because it carries no lawful route.

### State 2: In progress / layered

The card accumulates distinct layers contributed by the crew:

- Index: evidence / design-history trace;
- Silk: pressure-test / no-loophole mark;
- Writ: authority / mandate seal;
- Klause: selected-route marker;
- Rollback: recovery / restore / containment tab;
- Receipt: timestamp / audit mark.

### State 3: Programmed / scannable

The card is clean, readable, and final. It has layered marks, but not clutter. It should feel deliberately scoped, not universal.

## Vault design language

The vault should look robust, lawful, and serious. It may use clean metal forms, friendly-but-firm access panels, heavy rounded geometry, status lights, a keycard reader, and protective seals. It should not look evil, stupid, monstrous, or defeated.

## Payoff note

The retrieved item from the vault is currently planned as a handwritten note: **“If you're trying to access this, prove why.”** This turns the vault into a lesson-bearing surface rather than an enemy defeated by the heist.

## Palette

Use deep navy, charcoal, steel grey, soft off-white, controlled green approval, red rejection, amber caution, and small gold/cream authority accents. Do not make the keycard all teal; Patch identity colour should not dominate the prop.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Show a lawful vault and programmable override keycard system in the clean Adventures of Patch comic-heist style. The vault should look robust, serious, protected, and correctly resistant, with rounded heavy forms, a readable keycard reader, and clear status lights. The programmable override keycard should be a clean continuity object that can show states: blank or rejected, in-progress with distinct lawful layers, and final programmed/scannable. Its final layers should imply evidence, authority, selected route, pressure testing, rollback, and audit trail through simple readable marks, seals, strips, tabs, or stamps. The final scan should feel deliberately anticlimactic: card scans, light turns green, vault opens cleanly because the route is lawful.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not show a magic skeleton key, lockpick, crowbar, explosive breach, defeated evil vault, villain vault, hacker green-code wall, universal master key, criminal bypass, chaotic circuitry, or unreadable symbol clutter. Do not imply Patch broke, hacked, tricked, or defeated the vault. Do not make the keycard look like a generic credit card with no lawful layers, or like a magic object that works without evidence, authority, rollback, and audit. Do not overload the card with tiny unreadable text.
```

## QA hard gates

- Does the vault read as legitimate protection, not evil obstruction?
- Does the keycard state match the story beat?
- Is the keycard readable at slide scale?
- Are the lawful layers visible or inferable?
- Does the final access look scoped, not universal?
- Does the scene avoid bypass, breach, and hacker clichés?
- Is the final opening clean and anticlimactic rather than explosive?
