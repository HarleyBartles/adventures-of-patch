## Asset overview

Panel grammar controls how the heist world stages story beats. It is not a character or prop bible. Its job is to govern montage, sequence, cut-ins, and comic-book rhythm while preserving slide readability.

## Function and narrative role

The comic form should make the story feel fun and energetic without replacing the lesson. The recruitment montage creates momentum; the planning panels explain the lawful work; the final execution panel is deliberately anticlimactic.

## Current panel-language learnings

Use Candidate 1/2 panel mockup language as the deck-safe baseline: clean rectangular rhythm, readable sequence, and controlled variation. Use Candidate 3 for high-momentum heist montage moments. Use Candidate 4 sparingly for signature action-shaped panels where panel form itself expresses process.

Avoid arrows over panels. If direction is needed, use panel shape, panel placement, route continuity, lighting, and object motion.

## Panel types

### Lockout setup panel

Patch at the vault in normal or lightly stylised Patch mode. The vault refuses. The protected surface is doing its job.

### Heist reframe panel

Patch recognizes the problem as a heist job. This is the transition point into comic-book heist styling.

### Recruitment montage panels

Each specialist is introduced through their own domain and panel language.

### Planning sequence panels

Overhead table, close-up route marks, keycard layer additions, rejected paths, authority stamp, rollback tag, audit mark.

### Execution panel

Patch scans the final programmed card. Light turns green. Door opens. No drama.

### Celebration / takeaway panel

Return to canonical editorial vector. Crew celebrates in a lighter hideout. The visual point is relief, playfulness, and completeness, not spectacle.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Use clean comic-book panel grammar for the Override Heist while preserving Adventures of Patch readability. Use large readable panels, clear cause-and-effect staging, selective close-up inserts, simple caption boxes, motion accents, and graphic shadows. Recruitment panels should introduce specialists through their function and zone. Planning panels should show lawful layers being added to the override keycard. Execution panels should be deliberately anticlimactic: Patch scans the programmed keycard, the light turns green, and the vault opens cleanly. Keep panels readable at presentation scale.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not use chaotic comic-page overload, tiny unreadable panels, dense dialogue, excessive sound effects, manga-style clutter, chase sequences, gunfights, explosions, violent action climax, or spectacle that implies the danger is the point. Do not hide the key lesson inside busy panels. Do not merge recruitment and planning so the crew appears to skip the lawful design work.
```

## QA hard gates

- Are panels readable at slide scale?
- Does the sequence have clear cause and effect?
- Does panel grammar support the lesson rather than overwhelm it?
- Are recruitment and planning visually distinct?
- Is the final vault opening anticlimactic if this is the execution beat?
- Are text/caption elements short and legible?
- Are action effects controlled and meaningful?
