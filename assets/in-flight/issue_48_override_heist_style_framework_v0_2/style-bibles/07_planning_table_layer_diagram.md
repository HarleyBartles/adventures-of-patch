## Asset overview

The planning table is where the heist becomes lawful. It turns candidate routes into a scoped override keycard by layering evidence, pressure testing, authority, route selection, rollback, and audit.

## Function and narrative role

The planning table is the story’s teaching engine. Recruitment builds momentum, but the planning table shows why the final vault opening is legitimate.

## Layout language

Use readable process staging. Candidate routes may appear as cards, strings, paths, overlays, blueprint strips, or plan tiles. The visual flow should narrow from many possibilities to one selected route. The final keycard should sit at the center or end of the flow, receiving layers from each specialist.

## Recruitment grammar reuse

The planning-table slide should visually echo each recruitment language as an insert around the keycard:

- Index: paper-trail/document evidence layer.
- Silk: red-string route-testing / surviving path layer.
- Rollback: contingency/restore layer from bunker systems.
- Writ: formal authority/mandate layer.
- Klause: selected viable route layer.
- Receipt: audit slip/timestamp layer.

## Layer semantics

- Index: possible lawful routes from design history.
- Silk: rejects loopholes and unsafe shortcuts.
- Writ: rejects routes without authority.
- Klause: selects the route that will work.
- Rollback: adds abort conditions, restore route, containment.
- Receipt: records who/why/how/under whose authority.
- Patch: carries the final card to the vault.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Show an Override Heist planning-table scene in clean Adventures comic-heist style. The visual should make the lawful override process readable: candidate routes from design history narrow into one selected lawful route, while specialist contributions add distinct layers to a programmable override keycard. Use route cards, blueprint overlays, rejected-path marks, authority seals, pressure-test indicators, rollback/Plan B tags, audit stamps, and a central or final keycard object. Around the keycard, use small inserts that echo each recruit's panel grammar: archive paper for Index, red route test for Silk, containment system for Rollback, formal authority document for Writ, decision grid/stamp for Klause, and receipt slip for Receipt.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not show vague people merely looking at papers, an incomprehensible conspiracy wall, arbitrary glowing UI, tiny unreadable text, random strings everywhere, or one character solving everything alone. Do not skip authority, rollback, or audit. Do not make the planning look like criminal burglary planning, weapon selection, casino/bank robbery, or hacker bypass. Do not clutter the table so the selected route and keycard layers cannot be read.
```

## QA hard gates

- Does the scene visibly turn candidates into a selected route?
- Are specialist layers clear?
- Does the keycard gain or imply layers?
- Are authority, rollback, and audit included?
- Is the table readable at slide scale?
- Does the scene avoid vague paperwork and conspiracy-wall chaos?
- Does collaboration remain visible?
