## Asset overview

The safehouse is a comic-book heist HQ made of connected specialist zones. Each zone expresses one layer of lawful override design. The safehouse should feel coordinated but not generic. It is not a criminal lair; it is a lawful planning environment dressed in heist-world language.

## Function and narrative role

The safehouse makes the crew’s abstract functions visible. It hosts recruitment, specialist introductions, planning-table work, keycard programming, contingency planning, and audit capture.

## Shared environment language

Use connected corridors, alcoves, planning rooms, warm desk lamps, blueprint walls, test rigs, labelled trays, recovery lockers, route boards, vault diagrams, corkboards, worktables, cable runs, and clean safehouse signage. Maintain clear paths and readable spaces.

## Current world-style lead

The warm safehouse direction is the current lead for broad worldbuilding. It should feel cosy but operational: warm lighting, purposeful shelves and work surfaces, corridor alcoves, access hints, route boards, trays, lockers, and no criminal lair framing.

## Specialist zones

### Index zone: archive/design-history war room

Blueprints, revision overlays, old notebooks, sealed change logs, legal-route diagrams, research trails, file boxes, desk lamps, paper stacks, and evidence threads. It should feel like design history made navigable.

### Silk zone: red-team test corridor

Plain apartment-style entrance corridor retrofitted into a pressure-test corridor. Laser rig, mock alarm, pressure plates, tripwires, unsafe-shortcut tags, decoy readers, route strings, and warning marks. It should feel patient, precise, and adversarial without becoming horror.

### Rollback zone: recovery bunker / observation deck

Recovery and contingency space made physical. Observation deck, running containment simulation rooms, heavy glass, steel shutters, control panels, ropes, harnesses, med kits, spare power, backup drives, hard cases, foam sealant, circuit breakers, maps, extraction routes. Not a weapons armoury.

### Writ zone: Office of Lawful Standards / chambers

Old institutional authority environment: chambers, tribunal rooms, ledger halls, clerks with adding machines, departmental doors, frosted glass, seal desks, walnut, green leather, brass lamps, intercom, secretary, receipt pad, tea service.

### Klause zone: immaculate decision office

Minimal, disciplined, no-nonsense. Immaculate desk, appointment logic, aligned pen, negative space, severe grid, clean decision posture. The decision work is internal, not a physical sorting room.

### Receipt zone: audit alcove

A small easy-to-miss alcove off the corridor. Immaculate but cosy: warm desk lamp, labelled trays, timestamp clock, receipt printer, ledger-slate, pencil cup, small archive boxes, mug or tiny plant. It should feel precise, mild, and more important than it first appears.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Design a connected comic-book heist safehouse in the Adventures of Patch style: clean editorial-vector finish, thick rounded outlines, readable geometry, warm planning light, purposeful props, and clear specialist zones. The safehouse should feel like a lawful override planning HQ, not a criminal lair. Each zone should embody a different layer of the override process: design-history research, red-team testing, recovery/rollback planning, authority/mandate review, route-selection decision, and audit logging. Use short functional labels, blueprints, route boards, vault diagrams, labelled trays, recovery lockers, corridor alcoves, test rigs, and planning tables while keeping the composition readable at presentation scale.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not create a generic warehouse, casino, bank-robbery set, weapons cache, villain lair, hacker den, or random sci-fi control room. Do not put lasers, blueprints, screens, warning signs, and gadgets everywhere indiscriminately. Do not make every specialist zone look the same. Do not use irrelevant object stuffing, meaningless screens, dense illegible text, impossible room geometry, blocked paths without reason, random weapons, or dark clutter that hides the teaching point.
```

## QA hard gates

- Is the specialist zone function clear?
- Are props meaningful to the role?
- Is the layout readable and physically plausible?
- Are text labels short and purposeful?
- Are zones visually distinct from one another?
- Is clutter controlled?
- Is the Adventures style preserved?
