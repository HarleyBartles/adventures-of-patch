# Silk Character / Recruitment-Language Bible

Version: v0.3  
State: `minimal_provisional_character_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit recruitment / character-language bible  

This character bible locks approved recruitment-language doctrine for planning and pre-vis. It does not lock final character appearance, full turnaround design, final palette, or accepted asset-sheet canon.

## Function

Silk is the red-team / adversarial pressure-testing specialist. She tests whether Patch's proposed lawful route leaks, contains unsafe shortcuts, or collapses under hostile interpretation.

## Character posture

Silk is precise, tense, quiet, and adversarial without being villainous. She is spider-adjacent in motion and motif but must not become a literal spider, monster, or horror creature.

## Domain

Silk's domain is a red-team test corridor: narrow passages, tensioned red string, rappel drops, dead ends, switchbacks, crawl spaces, pressure plates, mock alarms, decoy readers, trap-like insets, blocked shortcuts, and final check pins.

For the current preferred frame-architecture direction, this test corridor should feel like an ordinary apartment corridor weaponised into a red-team test space: bare floorboards, plain plaster walls, cheap apartment doors, exposed conduit, taped wiring, bare hanging lightbulbs, utility cupboards, radiators, simple alarm boxes, maintenance hatches, improvised anchors, and small pressure-test devices.

Do not drift into cave, dungeon, bunker, sci-fi base, warehouse, cyberpunk maze, or horror labyrinth aesthetics.

## Panel grammar

Silk's panels are the physical route, but the route does not only run inside panels. It also runs through panel boundaries. The comic-page architecture itself is part of Silk's adversarial test corridor.

Use thin, edgy, risky panels linked by red string, but do not treat the string as a decorative overlay or a flat route map. The red string is a test rig: it anchors to panel corners, tensions broken borders, braces unsafe seams, marks failed crossings, holds gaps open, and proves which discontinuities can safely carry traversal.

Panels may contain corridor fragments, but the defining Silk grammar is broken-boundary traversal:

- a torn gutter can become a crawlspace;
- a cracked seam can become a squeeze gap;
- a split border can become a careful crossing between offset panel halves;
- a pulled-apart gutter can become a rappel or climb shaft;
- a fractured dead-end can prove that no passage exists;
- a tensioned red-string brace can hold a temporary route open.

Characters must not simply move inside one panel and then appear in another. When a route crosses panels, the crossing point must be visible as an opened, cracked, torn, bent, pulled-apart, or tensioned boundary. A body, hand, foot, rope, tool, or tension line should make the boundary traversal legible.

If a panel narrows, Patch and Silk squeeze through. If it drops, Silk rappels down. If it fractures, the break itself becomes the traversal challenge. If it dead-ends, they stop and reroute. If a switchback gutter is needed, it is a real crawlspace or test-corridor bypass through the page architecture.

The route should feel unsafe before testing and readable only after Silk pressure-tests it. Silk does not merely discover a shortcut; she proves which broken seams, gutters, cracks, and boundary gaps hold under adversarial interpretation. The final route is not shorter; it is safer.

## Central shaft rule

When a central shaft layout is used, the shaft is a panel-boundary traversal feature, not an ordinary room, stairwell, ladder well, or platform-game shaft.

The shaft must have:
- one clear entry break;
- one clear exit break;
- visible traversal logic between them.

For the current preferred central-shaft composition, the shaft entry is at the lower-left edge of the shaft and the shaft exit is at the upper-right edge of the shaft. Avoid vague extra shaft breaches or floating access points that weaken the route logic.

## Recruitment journey

Patch arrives and asks his ask. Silk gives little away, clips onto a line, and rappels off into the test corridor. Patch follows more casually, still pitching and clarifying. The route is tested in motion. Patch's proposal only earns attention when it survives the corridor.

## Payoff mechanism

The route reaches the final check-mark pin. The pin tag already says:

**I’m in**

Silk is surprised. A caption, not a thought bubble or speech bubble, says:

**You son of a gun.**

She hands Patch the **I’m in** tag.

## Positive constraint block

```text
Silk is a Patch-level autonomous-agent red-team pressure tester. Her recruitment slide is an adversarial route traversal through a thin, tense, red-string apartment-corridor test route. Panels are physical corridor segments and broken page-boundaries: narrow slivers, torn gutters, cracked seams, squeeze gaps, rappel shafts, crawl-through borders, blocked shortcuts, dead ends, tensioned red-string braces, reroutes, and final check pins. Patch asks his ask and follows while pitching; Silk rappels ahead and pressure-tests the route. Every panel transition visibly crosses the broken boundary itself rather than merely jumping from one panel to another. The route survives only when it reaches a final check-mark pin whose tag says “I’m in.” Silk is surprised; a caption says “You son of a gun.” She hands Patch the tag.
```

## Negative constraint block

```text
Do not make Silk a literal spider, animal, monster, horror creature, human woman, Patch clone, Bit/Bot-style robot, or villain. Do not add extra arms, mandibles, spider abdomen, spider legs, fangs, horror webbing, or predatory menace. Do not make the panels decorative only; the characters must physically traverse the panel route and its broken boundaries. Do not let characters simply move inside one panel and appear in another without crossing the visible seam, gutter, crack, split, or tensioned break. Do not use classical left-to-right panels if the test route should divert, fracture, climb, or reroute. Do not turn the corridor into a cave, dungeon, bunker, sci-fi base, warehouse, cyberpunk maze, or horror labyrinth.
```

## QA gates

- Does the panel layout function as the actual route?
- Are panel boundaries themselves traversable, not merely decorative dividers?
- Does each panel-to-panel crossing have a visible opened, cracked, torn, bent, pulled-apart, or tensioned boundary?
- Do characters visibly cross through the seam, gutter, crack, split, or boundary gap rather than simply appearing in adjacent panels?
- Are dead ends and reroutes readable but not chaotic?
- Is Silk spider-adjacent without being a spider?
- Does Patch physically follow the test route?
- Does the environment remain recognisably ordinary and domestic rather than cave, dungeon, bunker, sci-fi base, warehouse, cyberpunk maze, or horror labyrinth?
- If a central shaft is used, does it have one clear entry break and one clear exit break, with no vague extra breaches?
- Does the final check-pin tag say “I’m in”?
- Does the “You son of a gun” caption land as surprise?
