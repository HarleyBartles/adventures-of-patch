# Rollback Character / Recruitment-Language Bible


State: `minimal_provisional_character_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit recruitment / character-language bible  

This character bible locks approved recruitment-language doctrine for planning and pre-vis. It does not lock final character appearance, full turnaround design, final palette, or accepted asset-sheet canon.


## Function

Rollback is the recovery, contingency, containment, abort-condition, restore-path, and Plan B specialist. He proves that a plan without recovery is not a plan.

## Character posture

Rollback is broad, physically imposing, Black, calm under pressure, practical, blunt, wry, and veteran-coded without being gun-happy or gung-ho. He is not a weapons character. His confidence comes from controlled preparedness.

## Domain

Rollback's domain is an active contingency bunker. It is not a static storage room. It has an observation deck overlooking running containment experiments and disaster simulations. The bunker is subtly too prepared.

Rooms begin with normal labels such as **Plan A** and **Plan B**, then jump to things like **Plan M**, then lose tidy alphabetic designation and become increasingly elaborate failure-on-failure scenarios:

- Recovery After Recovery Failure
- If The Backup Door Is Also The Problem
- Partial Containment With Wet Floor
- Everything Catches Fire But Politely
- If The Clever Bit Fails Twice
- If The Escape Route Needs Escaping From

## Panel grammar

Rollback's panels are chunky, load-bearing observation-window panels. They feel like reinforced bunker architecture: heavy glass, steel shutters, control panels, thick borders, containment doors, hazard margins, and stable infrastructure.

The slide is a linear inspection route along the observation deck. The room progression is the recruitment slide progression.

## Recruitment journey

Patch follows Rollback along the observation deck while pitching. They pass active containment simulations. **Plan A** is catastrophically failing and must be locked down. Rollback barely reacts; he locks it down and a steel shutter snaps over the observation window.

Other rooms are stable and controlled. The sequence demonstrates that Rollback assumes Plan A failure is normal, while Patch has not brought even Plan B.

Rollback stops Patch:

**What’s your Plan B?**

Patch has none.

Rollback:

**Then you ain’t got no plan.**

The implied “fool” is felt, not spoken.

## Realization and payoff

Rollback's realization is not that Patch understands contingency. It is:

**That’s why you need me.**

He concedes with wry inevitability:

**You son of a gun.**

He slams a bunker control button. Every monitor and observation window flips to:

**I’M IN**

Rollback immediately walks on toward the next corridor of simulations, leaving Patch standing alone in a room glowing with synchronized **I’M IN** screens.

## Positive constraint block

```text
Rollback is a Patch-level autonomous-agent contingency and recovery specialist. His recruitment slide is a linear inspection along an active contingency bunker observation deck. Patch follows while pitching. Each panel is a running containment simulation behind glass. Plan A is catastrophically failing and Rollback calmly locks it down behind a steel shutter; other rooms are stable, controlled backup scenarios. Room labels escalate from Plan A, Plan B, Plan M, into increasingly imaginative failure-on-failure contingencies. Rollback asks “What’s your Plan B?” and, when Patch has none, says “Then you ain’t got no plan.” He realizes this is exactly why Patch needs him, says “You son of a gun,” hits a control button, all observation windows flip to “I’M IN,” and he walks on.
```

## Negative constraint block

```text
Do not make Rollback a literal human soldier, gun-happy commando, weapons dealer, villain, bodyguard stereotype, animal, Patch clone, Bit/Bot-style robot, or generic strongman. Do not show weapons racks, tactical violence, war scenes, flags, real military insignia, or aggressive combat posing. Do not make every simulation chaotic; only Plan A is catastrophically failing. Do not make the bunker a static storeroom instead of an active observation deck.
```

## QA gates

- Does the room progression carry the recruitment slide?
- Is Plan A the only catastrophic failure?
- Are the backup simulations controlled and increasingly imaginative?
- Does the bunker read as recovery/contingency, not weapons/military fantasy?
- Does Rollback's realization read as “that’s why you need me”?
- Does he leave Patch in the glowing “I’M IN” screen room?
