# Stakeholder Dugout Asset Pack Style Bible

**Project:** *Adventures of Patch*  
**Adventure issue:** #56  
**Asset pack:** Stakeholder dugout / route-check booth  
**Reference basis:** Derived from the attached stakeholder dugout images in this conversation. Not live repo-verified in this pass.

## 1. Pack Identity

The stakeholder dugout is a semi-official field station on the long-course route. It is not a grand judging pavilion and not a casual picnic shelter. It sits between bureaucracy, race control, stakeholder observation, and field-note collection.

The visual identity should read as:

- warm outdoor tournament infrastructure;
- practical but carefully branded;
- slightly improvised, made of timber, canvas, crates, clipboards, maps, notes, ledgers, and route materials;
- stakeholder-facing rather than competitor-facing;
- a calm checkpoint where ambiguity is documented, compared, and reasoned through.

The dugout should feel like a place where people have been sitting all morning checking route choices, debating evidence, marking decisions, and keeping paper trails.

## 2. Relationship to the Wider Issue #56 Visual System

This pack belongs to the same tournament world as the environment, officials, and canonical costume packs. It should preserve the cheerful blue-and-gold event branding, rolling countryside, route barriers, white trophy/laurel insignia, and warm daylight editorial look.

Where the wider environment assets are broad and scenic, the stakeholder dugout is denser, more paper-driven, and more procedural. It should still feel friendly and readable, but with a higher concentration of documents, maps, labels, route notes, checklists, pens, crates, and pinned references.

Patch can appear here as a participant/agent seeking clarification, but the dugout itself remains the key asset. Stakeholders are helpful observers and process-keepers, not judges in formal cream uniforms.

## 3. Core Visual Motifs

Use these recurring visual motifs consistently:

- Blue canvas awning with white trophy/laurel branding.
- Weathered wooden booth structure with benches, tables, crates, shelves, and pinned boards.
- Route map pinned to the back wall, usually with a winding blue course line.
- Clipboards labelled or visually coded as route notes, rules, decision sheets, checklists, map notes, and quick notes.
- Spiral notebook pages, handwritten arrows, question marks, circled checks, wrong-route dashed lines, and “stay on path” reminders.
- Blue-and-white event flags and barriers visible outside.
- Rolling green hills, lake, dirt route, and distant course fencing beyond the booth.
- Paper clutter that is organized enough to be readable at a glance.

Avoid making the dugout look like a trophy-award table or judge’s podium. Its job is route interpretation and stakeholder process, not medals or prize ceremony.

## 4. Environment Design

The booth sits beside the long course, usually near a dirt track that continues into rolling green countryside. It should have a partial open-air construction: timber posts, canvas roof, open front or side, and a clear view of the route outside.

The immediate setting should include wildflowers, grass tufts, rocks, rope stakes, blue route barriers, white flags, and a bright sky with large soft clouds. The area is maintained but rural, with tournament infrastructure laid over a natural landscape.

The booth interior should be wood-heavy: benches, trestle tables, crates, shelves, pinned wall boards, clipboards, ledgers, and boxes. Surfaces can be scuffed and practical. The booth should feel well-used, not pristine.

## 5. Map and Paper Language

Maps are central to this asset pack. The route map language should distinguish between official route diagrams and hand-scribbled reasoning notes.

Official booth maps:

- use green terrain, blue water, pale roads, and a bold blue course line;
- are pinned flat to the wall or clipped to boards;
- may include route labels, small icons, checkpoints, and mini inset maps;
- should be clean enough to read as official without needing legible microtext.

Hand-scribbled maps:

- appear on lined notebook paper or sketchbook pages;
- use teal or blue-green for the true route;
- use dashed blue lines and question marks for tempting or wrong paths;
- include annotations such as “stay on path,” “do not cut across,” “not the finish,” “watch it,” or equivalent short route warnings;
- should look handmade, anxious, and practical, not polished.

When text is used, keep it large and selective. Do not overload the image with tiny pseudo-writing that distracts from the asset purpose.

## 6. Stakeholder Character Treatment

Stakeholders in this pack are friendly, competent, civilian-process people rather than formal race officials. Their clothing should be more varied and less uniform than judge/official costumes.

Typical stakeholder cues:

- glasses, lanyards, ID badges, casual shirts, cardigans, blazers, or smart-casual layers;
- clipboards, notebooks, pens, ledgers, route paperwork;
- engaged expressions: curious, thoughtful, helpful, comparing notes;
- body language leaning over the table, pointing at route notes, checking documents, or listening to Patch.

They should not look like stern authorities. They are part of the stakeholder process: documenting, discussing, clarifying, and helping turn ambiguous route evidence into a reasonable decision.

## 7. Patch Integration

When Patch appears, preserve the canonical tournament costume from the associated costume pack: teal hoodie with antenna hood, white/cream bib layer, race number 042, black shoulder bag with terminal prompt mark, teal shoes, simple friendly face, and rounded readable proportions.

Patch’s role in this setting is investigative and note-taking. Preferred actions include:

- holding a spiral notebook;
- writing route observations;
- listening to stakeholders;
- presenting notes;
- standing beside the booth table without dominating it;
- appearing curious, attentive, or mildly uncertain.

Patch should not be treated as a judge or booth owner. Patch is the participant-agent navigating ambiguity.

## 8. Composition Patterns

Useful compositions for this pack:

1. Establishing view: booth on the right side of the image, long route and landscape opening to the left or background.
2. Interior stakeholder moment: Patch at the side of the table with stakeholders gathered around documents and route maps.
3. Detail table view: close-up of route board, clipboards, notes, pens, stamps, ledgers, crates, and blue branded materials.
4. Notebook/map insert: hand-scribbled route map on lined paper, showing true route, false temptations, warnings, checks, and question marks.

Keep strong depth cues. The booth should feel anchored beside the course, not floating on a white background unless the requested asset is specifically an isolated paper prop.

## 9. Colour Palette

The pack uses the wider tournament palette but with more rustic wood and paper:

- event blue: awnings, banners, table runners, clipboards, route barriers, lanyards;
- cream and parchment: canvas roof undersides, notebooks, forms, pinned papers;
- warm wood: booth structure, benches, crates, tables, notice boards;
- natural greens: hills, grass, trees, hedges, flowers;
- teal: Patch and hand-drawn true-route markings;
- white: flags, event insignia, paper highlights;
- gold accents: trophy/laurel marks, finials, small trim.

Avoid heavy red except where it marks warnings, wrong routes, hazard cues, or small tournament accents. Red should not overpower the booth’s blue-and-gold identity.

## 10. Lighting and Rendering

Lighting should be bright outdoor daylight with soft shadows. Interior booth scenes can be shaded by the canvas roof, but still warm and legible. Use painterly shading with clean vector-adjacent linework, consistent with the rest of the Issue #56 tournament assets.

The booth should have a slightly tactile texture: wood grain, canvas creases, paper edges, scuffed notebooks, pinned corners, pencil marks, and worn blue fabric. Keep details readable and charming rather than photorealistic.

## 11. Prop Continuity

Important recurring props:

- large pinned route map;
- long-course hand-scribbled notebook map;
- route notes clipboard;
- rules clipboard;
- decision sheet clipboard;
- checklists and quick notes;
- blue notebooks or binders with trophy/laurel insignia;
- pens, pencils, cups, clips, folders, crates, rolled maps;
- lanyards, small badges, stamps, and check boxes;
- blue branded cloth/banner panels hanging from the booth.

Props should communicate procedure and evidence. They should feel like tools for interpreting the route, not generic desk clutter.

## 12. Typography and Symbols

Use short, readable uppercase labels for primary signs and boards:

- ROUTE
- RULES
- NOTES
- CHECKLIST
- DECISION SHEET
- MAP
- QUICK NOTES
- NOTES

For notebook annotations, use loose handwritten text with limited phrases. Symbols should be clearer than prose: arrows, check marks, X marks, question marks, dashed temptation paths, circles around key route points, and simple warning sketches.

The trophy/laurel emblem should remain the main official brand mark. It can appear on awnings, cloth banners, notebooks, barriers, and route flags.

## 13. What to Preserve

Preserve these elements across future stakeholder dugout assets:

- blue canvas and white trophy/laurel booth branding;
- rustic timber field-station construction;
- route map and paperwork density;
- long-course countryside visible outside;
- stakeholder process atmosphere: discussion, notes, checks, route interpretation;
- Patch as curious participant when present;
- warm daylight and readable editorial clarity.

## 14. What to Avoid

Avoid:

- turning the dugout into a formal judging stand or awards podium;
- overusing medals, trophies, or ceremony props;
- making stakeholders look like strict officials in identical uniforms;
- making the booth too modern, corporate, or sterile;
- using dense illegible text as the main visual hook;
- making the route map too abstract to read as a course map;
- replacing the rural long-course setting with an indoor office;
- crowding Patch or stakeholders so much that the booth asset is lost.

## 16. Asset-Pack Summary

The stakeholder dugout asset pack defines the adventure’s procedural heart: a friendly but paper-heavy checkpoint where route ambiguity is observed, recorded, discussed, and clarified. It should visually bridge the long-course environment, the stakeholder theme, and Patch’s reasoning work. The core mood is not judgment or victory; it is evidence, notes, route discipline, and reasonable interpretation in a sunny tournament field.
