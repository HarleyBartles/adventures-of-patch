# Judges / Officials Asset Pack Style Bible

Project: *Adventures of Patch*

## 1. Purpose of the Pack

This asset pack defines the visual language for tournament authority figures and their supporting objects in *Patch and the Tournament of Reasonable Defaults*. The pack should make judging feel warm, trustworthy, readable, and lightly ceremonial rather than bureaucratic, severe, or comic-villainous.

The judges and officials are not antagonists. They are competent stewards of a cheerful old-fashioned athletic tournament. Their role is to validate attempts, record outcomes, manage medals and release cards, and make the event feel orderly without making it feel cold.

These assets should be reusable across deck scenes, source sheets, prompt boards, and continuity references. Each image should be visually clear enough to support later production, cropping, and asset-sheet compilation.

## 2. Core Visual Identity

The style is a clean, editorial, vector-adjacent storybook look with light painterly shading. Forms are simplified and readable, with confident dark linework, rounded shapes, warm daylight, and practical source-asset clarity.

The world is an old-fashioned countryside tournament: green rolling hills, wildflowers, blue skies, cream-and-red tents, bunting, brass posts, rope barriers, banners, tablecloths, clipboards, medals, stamps, cards, and whistles. It should feel like a well-run village sports day crossed with a gentle fantasy-adventure institution.

The key emotional tone is cheerful competence. Officials should look approachable, alert, and fair. Props should look official but handmade enough to belong in a friendly outdoor tournament rather than a corporate process.

## 3. Palette

Primary colours:

- Deep tournament blue: banners, tablecloths, lanyards, ribbons, cards, borders, and official branding.
- Warm gold: trophy marks, laurel emblems, medals, banner trim, brass posts, stamp bases, and ceremonial accents.
- Cream / parchment: polo shirts, cards, checklists, envelopes, paper forms, signboards, and tent panels.
- Khaki / tan: official trousers, dusty paths, wooden boards, clipboard backing, crates, and neutral grounding.
- Warm countryside greens: grass, hedges, distant hills, flowers, and environmental depth.
- Small red / white / yellow accents: bunting, flags, wax seals, route markers, and medal-ribbon contrast.

Avoid harsh neon, black-heavy costumes, corporate greys, sterile whites, or saturated modern sports branding. The palette should stay sunny, warm, and slightly nostalgic.

## 4. Lighting and Atmosphere

Lighting should be warm daylight with open blue sky and soft, friendly shadows. Shadows are present but not dramatic. The scene should feel outdoors, clear, and readable.

Common atmospheric elements:

- Bright blue sky with soft cumulus clouds.
- Rolling green hills and tree lines.
- Dirt paths or packed tournament ground.
- Small wildflowers at the edges of the frame.
- Bunting strung between brass or gold-toned posts.
- Light breeze implied through flags and banners.

Avoid night scenes, storm lighting, theatrical spotlights, fog, heavy bloom, dramatic rim lighting, or grim arena atmosphere unless an issue-specific scene explicitly calls for a tonal shift.

## 5. Linework, Shape Language, and Rendering

Linework is clean, rounded, and confident. The images should look polished and illustration-led rather than painterly-realistic. Edges may have slight texture but should remain crisp enough for asset extraction.

Shape language:

- Officials: rounded, approachable faces; expressive eyes; open posture; practical uniforms.
- Props: simplified silhouettes; iconic shapes; clear function at first glance.
- Environment: soft hills, curved tent forms, rounded posts, banners with neat fabric folds.

Rendering uses light painterly shading and subtle texture. Fabric can show gentle folds and cloth grain. Metal should have soft highlights rather than chrome shine. Paper should feel cream, slightly warm, and tactile.

## 6. Character Design: Officials and Judges

Officials wear coordinated tournament attire rather than military, school, or corporate uniforms.

Common uniform elements:

- Cream short-sleeve polo shirt.
- Tournament crest badge on chest: deep blue shield or patch with gold trophy/laurel motif.
- Khaki trousers with belt.
- Brown practical shoes or boots unless a specific continuity correction changes them.
- Blue lanyard, whistle, badge, or medal-official pass.
- Optional blue-and-gold sash for senior officials or ceremonial judging roles.
- Clipboard, checklist, medal, flag, envelope, or stamp as role-defining prop.

Officials should vary by age, face, gender presentation, hair, and role while staying in the same uniform family. Age variety is useful: older head judges communicate experience; younger medal officials communicate operational support. Facial expressions should be gentle, attentive, or mildly proud, not smug or severe.

Do not add Patch, Bit, Bot, contestants, spectators, stakeholders, hands-only human intrusions, or extra characters unless the specific asset request asks for them. This pack is about judges, officials, and their objects.

## 7. Role Archetypes

### Route Official

The route official manages course validation, checkpoint signage, and safe progress through a marked trial route.

Visual cues:

- Holding a small blue tournament flag.
- Clipboard or route checklist.
- Whistle on lanyard.
- Rope barrier, route sign, and checkpoint table nearby.
- Standing near a path, marker sign, or roped course entrance.

Mood: helpful, calm, ready to direct participants.

### Head Judge

The head judge is the senior authority figure. They embody fairness, institutional memory, and calm oversight.

Visual cues:

- Older official with confident stance.
- Blue-and-gold sash over cream shirt.
- Clipboard held at side or against torso.
- Medal table, podium blocks, banner, or official table nearby.
- Slightly more formal posture than route officials.

Mood: dignified but kind; not authoritarian.

### Medal Official

The medal official handles the award moment and the physical medals.

Visual cues:

- Holding or presenting a medal.
- Medal tray or award table in foreground.
- Medal-official badge or lanyard.
- Nearby validated sign, envelope, stamp, checklist, whistle, or pencil cup.
- Blue-and-gold medal ribbons.

Mood: proud, encouraging, precise.

### Awards Table / Judging Table

The awards table is a reusable environmental-prop surface that gathers the pack’s object language.

Visual cues:

- Deep blue tablecloth with large gold trophy/laurel emblem.
- Divided work zones, optionally separated by thin gold lines.
- Clipboards, pencils, cards, stamp, flags, whistle, medals, sealed envelope, and validation forms.
- Tournament background behind the table: tents, banners, bunting, hills, route lanes.

Mood: ordered, prepared, friendly, ceremonial.

## 8. Official Props and Object Language

The props should remain simple, iconic, and extractable. They can appear as a sheet or individually isolated objects.

Core objects:

- Clipboard with checklists and ticks.
- Thin rule cards or checklist cards.
- Decision cards: green check, yellow hold/circle, red cross.
- Wooden stamp and blue stamp pad.
- Yellow pencils in a tournament cup.
- Whistle with blue cord or lanyard.
- Small table flags in blue, white, and red.
- Blue lanyard with official badge.
- Sealed gold envelope with red wax tournament seal.
- Medal tray with gold, silver, and bronze medals.
- Small tent-card sign reading validation or award-release status.
- Wooden chest or crate with tournament mark.

Text on props should be minimal and mostly symbolic. When readable text is needed, use short functional labels such as “VALIDATED”, “AWARD RELEASE CARD”, or “MEDAL OFFICIAL”. Avoid long paragraphs, real-world legalistic forms, or tiny pseudo-text that becomes visual noise.

## 9. Tournament Branding

The recurring brand mark is a gold trophy with laurel branches, usually on deep blue. It appears on:

- Banners.
- Tablecloth fronts.
- Shirt crests.
- Clipboards and forms.
- Medal trays.
- Stamps.
- Lanyard badges.
- Envelopes or wax seals.
- Wooden chests.

The mark should be consistent in spirit, not necessarily identical in microscopic detail. Keep it simple: trophy silhouette, laurel curve, shield or circular framing where useful. It should read at small scale.

Blue-and-gold is the authority palette. Red/white/yellow should be supporting accent colours for route marking, flags, decision states, and tent fabric.

## 10. Environment Rules

The tournament setting is bright countryside rather than a stadium. Environmental continuity should include:

- Dirt or gravel path.
- Rolling hills and soft tree lines.
- Blue sky and white clouds.
- Red-and-cream striped tents.
- Blue banners with gold trophy/laurel marks.
- Gold/brass posts with rope or fabric bunting.
- Wildflowers and grass around the course.
- Wooden tables, crates, or chests.

The setting should be populated by visual infrastructure, not crowds. Background figures, if included at all, should be distant, indistinct, and non-distracting. For source assets, it is usually better to keep the scene clear and object-focused.

## 11. Composition Guidance

### Character asset compositions

Use three-quarter or front-facing views with full-body or three-quarter body framing. Keep the official readable as a reusable asset. One hand should usually hold the role-defining prop; the other may rest on a clipboard, table, medal, flag, or hip.

Good character framing:

- Official centered or slightly off-center.
- Tournament banner or table supporting role identity.
- Clear silhouette with no major object tangent through the face or torso.
- Feet visible when full-body continuity matters.
- Prop readable at thumbnail size.

Avoid over-crowding the official with too many props. One primary prop and one support surface are usually enough.

### Table and prop compositions

Use straight-on or mild three-quarter tabletop views. Arrange props in tidy clusters with clear negative space between them. Keep individual silhouettes separable.

Good table framing:

- Strong horizontal table surface.
- Emblem centered on tablecloth front.
- Prop groups arranged by function: recording, validation, signaling, awards.
- Background course visible but softened behind the main prop line.

Avoid extreme perspective, cluttered piles, unreadable micro-objects, and cropped-off primary items unless the image is intentionally a scene rather than a source sheet.

## 12. Continuity Rules

Maintain these continuity points across related outputs:

- Officials wear cream shirts and khaki trousers.
- Tournament crest is blue and gold with trophy/laurel motif.
- Main ceremonial textiles are deep blue with gold trim.
- Medal ribbons are blue with gold stripes or blue/gold dominant.
- The tournament is outdoors in warm daylight.
- Course infrastructure uses brass/gold posts, ropes, bunting, banners, and tented stations.
- Props use cream paper, blue/gold branding, and simple validation symbols.
- Official mood remains fair, friendly, and competent.

Do not drift into modern corporate event staff, school sports referees, police/security, medieval guild bureaucracy, militarized adjudicators, or grim fantasy tournament judges.

## 13. Do / Don’t

Do:

- Keep officials approachable and competent.
- Use warm daylight and readable silhouettes.
- Keep blue-and-gold branding consistent.
- Use symbolic checklists, cards, medals, stamps, and route flags.
- Make props clean enough for later extraction or asset-sheet use.
- Preserve the countryside tournament setting.
- Let ceremonial details feel handmade and friendly.

Don’t:

- Add Patch, Bit, Bot, contestants, spectators, stakeholders, or extra characters to official-only source assets.
- Make officials stern, villainous, militarized, corporate, or overly bureaucratic.
- Use crowded text-heavy forms.
- Replace the blue-and-gold tournament identity with unrelated team colours.
- Turn medals, trophies, or forms into generic clip-art without tournament branding.
- Overload one image with every prop unless it is explicitly a prop reference sheet.
- Use photorealism, anime exaggeration, heavy impasto painting, or flat sterile vector art.

## 14. Prompt-Building Pattern

Use this structure for future prompts in this asset family:

> Create a clean editorial source image for the judges/officials asset pack in *Patch and the Tournament of Reasonable Defaults*. Use the same simplified Adventures editorial / clean vector-adjacent style as the accepted tournament officials assets: cheerful old-fashioned countryside athletic tournament, warm daylight, blue-and-gold event branding, rounded confident linework, simplified readable forms, light painterly shading, practical reusable source-asset clarity. Show [specific official or prop] as [composition]. Include [role-specific props]. Keep the scene friendly, fair, and well-organized. Use deep blue textiles with gold trophy/laurel branding, cream paper/forms, khaki official uniform elements, brass posts, bunting, green hills, and red-and-cream tournament tents where appropriate. Do not include Patch, Bit, Bot, contestants, spectators, hands, or extra characters unless explicitly requested.

## 15. Negative Prompt / Avoidance Language

Use when the generator is likely to drift:

> Avoid photorealism, gritty fantasy, harsh modern sports branding, corporate conference styling, military/security uniforms, police imagery, school referee stripes, grim authority, hostile expressions, cluttered unreadable paperwork, excessive tiny text, neon colours, flat icon-only vector art, collage layouts, multiple numbered panels, extra characters, Patch, Bit, Bot, spectators, contestants, stakeholders, or hands entering the frame.

## 16. Asset Acceptance Checklist

Before accepting a new judges/officials asset, check:

- Does it clearly belong to the same blue-and-gold tournament world?
- Is the official or object’s role readable without explanation?
- Is the mood fair, warm, competent, and non-threatening?
- Are the forms simplified and extractable enough for source-asset use?
- Are the props consistent with the existing official pack?
- Is the environment sunny countryside tournament rather than stadium, office, or fantasy court?
- Are there no unwanted Patch/Bit/Bot/contestant/spectator additions?
- Is any text minimal, functional, and legible enough not to distract?
- Does the image preserve the accepted editorial/vector-adjacent style with light painterly shading?
