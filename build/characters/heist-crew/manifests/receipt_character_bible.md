# Receipt Character / Recruitment-Language Bible

State: `accepted_hero_and_style_sheet_visual_anchor_plus_recruitment_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit recruitment / character-language bible

This character bible locks approved recruitment-language doctrine for planning and pre-vis and now records the accepted Receipt hero and v1 style-sheet visual anchor. It does not by itself store the generated image asset files; use the accepted hero/style-sheet artifacts from the current visual workflow as the visual source images.

## Function

Receipt is the audit-trail, record, timestamp, reviewability, and proof-after-the-fact specialist. He ensures the override is not an invisible backdoor.

## Character posture

Receipt is neat, mild, precise, nerdy, quiet, and easy to underestimate. He is the anti-spectacle recruit. His power is that he has already been observing and recording everything before anyone noticed.

Receipt should feel like an older, careful, long-serving audit clerk translated into Patch-level autonomous-agent form. He is calm, exact, patient, and mildly tired rather than theatrical, anxious, fussy, smug, or comic-relief-only. His authority is not loud; it comes from the fact that the record already exists.

## Domain

Receipt's domain is a tiny audit alcove: receipt printer, paper rolls, timestamp clock, labelled trays, carbon copy, ledger-slate, pencil cup, stamp pad, small archive boxes, warm desk lamp, tidy drawers, and tiny filing rituals.

His tools are simple, seemingly outdated, and reliable because the audit job has not changed since he started doing it. They should look ordinary, trusted, and still in service, not whimsical antiques, decorative steampunk props, magic objects, or a clutter pile. In hero imagery, use only the props he is holding or directly using. In style-sheet kit/prop rows, show the full domain toolkit cleanly and canonically.

## Accepted hero and style-sheet visual anchor

The accepted Receipt direction is a compact, older Patch-level autonomous-agent audit-trail specialist holding or issuing an audit receipt. He is character-first, not an environment study or prop study.

Locked visual traits:

- Patch-level autonomous agent, not human, animal, mole, furry, helper bot, Bit/Bot-style robot, Patch clone, or generic office worker;
- compact, short, narrow, stooped older-gent clerk silhouette;
- mild, precise, quiet, tired-but-calm posture;
- one single stubby antenna on top of the head, small and understated;
- one continuous curved pale aqua / glass-like faceplate forming the whole face, including chin and jawline;
- no faceplate partition seams, metal chin strap, lower rim, helmet-window read, mouthplate, or separate lower face panel;
- simple dark tired eye marks and tiny mild mouth on or within the glass faceplate;
- white upper head casing that softly reads like white old-gent hair but is not literal hair;
- white casing should stay primarily on the upper head/crown, not become white side ears or broad white cheek panels;
- side hardware, if present, is subtle and subdued, not ear-like or dominant;
- simple soft agent hands, not exposed mechanical fingers, paws, claws, or human hands;
- maroon knitted tank top / vest;
- cream shirt;
- narrow dark tie;
- tidy dark brown / charcoal trousers;
- small polished dark shoes;
- audit receipt slip as the key held prop/action object;
- clean Adventures editorial-vector finish: thick rounded outlines, simplified readable forms, soft controlled shading, warm paper-like background, and character-first composition.

The accepted v1 style sheet additionally locks a controlled domain-prop set: audit receipt slip, receipt roll/printer, timestamp clock, carbon copy slips, ledger-slate, pencil cup, verified/date stamp, stamp pad, labelled trays, and archive box. These belong in canonical kit/props and purposeful action poses, not as background clutter in a hero portrait.

## Face and head construction lock

Receipt's face is a single curved glass-like faceplate, not a helmet visor. The faceplate includes the full face area down through the chin and jawline. Avoid any construction that looks like a visor set into a helmet, a metal chin strap, a lower robot mouthplate, or a separate jaw component.

The white upper casing is an age-code and silhouette cue. It may read like white hair from shape, value, and fine surface strokes, but it is casing, not human hair. Keep it mainly above the faceplate. Do not wrap it around the cheeks as white side ears or animal/human side panels.

The single stubby antenna is part of the shared autonomous-agent read. It must stay subtle and Receipt-specific: no paired antennae, no Patch-style teal feelers, no playful bug/insect read, and no expressive bouncing antenna acting as the main personality cue.

## Clothing lock

Receipt's clothing is neat, old-fashioned, and well kept. The maroon knitted vest over cream shirt and narrow dark tie is the core clothing identity. He should look orderly and long-serving, not shabby, tramp-like, banker-formal, corporate-modern, fashionable, sporty, tactical, or costume-like.

Avoid adding visor and sleeve garters in the first locked hero/style-sheet state. They may be future situational accents, but the accepted v1 design uses the simpler vest, shirt, tie, trousers, and polished shoes read.

## Action / utility pose language

Receipt's actions should communicate quiet proof and anti-recruitment, not broad comic action.

Canonical style-sheet action poses:

1. **Hand audit slip** — Receipt offers the confirmed audit slip forward.
2. **Polite interruption / no pitch needed** — Receipt alone gently raises one soft agent hand in a courteous stop gesture while already holding or preparing the audit slip. This should read as “I must interrupt; there is really no need for a pitch,” without showing Patch, a speech bubble, rudeness, argument, or theatrical authority.
3. **Tear receipt** — Receipt tears the proof slip from a receipt roll/printer.
4. **Stamp confirmed** — Receipt applies a verified/date stamp.
5. **File carbon copy** — Receipt files the duplicate into a labelled tray.
6. **Check timestamp / ledger-slate** — Receipt quietly verifies the record/time against a small slate or audit card.

The polite interruption pose is a key character lock. It should stay focused on Receipt's mild, precise, already-prepared body language, not become a two-character Patch interaction.

## Recruitment structure

Receipt is the anti-recruitment. Patch arrives ready to pitch, but Receipt already knows. He has quietly recorded every prior recruitment angle: provenance, adversarial test, contingency, authority, and decision.

He simply shakes his head, tears off the receipt, and hands it to Patch.

## Payoff mechanism

The receipt records the observed canonical assent format:

- realization;
- “you son of a gun”;
- “I’m in.”

Writ's “you sly old bombardier” is logged as a lawful equivalent. Receipt reproduces the canonical format correctly because he has observed and recorded the pattern.

The receipt includes:

**YOU SON OF A GUN**  
**I’M IN**

with audit notation that the observed format is complete.
