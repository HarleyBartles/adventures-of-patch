## Function

Rollback is the recovery, contingency, containment, abort-condition, restore-path, and Plan B specialist. He proves that a plan without recovery is not a plan.

He is preparedness made into a person. His confidence comes from controlled recovery logic, not aggression.

## Character posture

Rollback is broad, physically imposing, Black, calm under pressure, practical, blunt, wry, and veteran-coded without being gun-happy or gung-ho. He is not a weapons character. His confidence comes from controlled preparedness.

Rollback should feel like he has already considered three failure states beyond the one everyone else can see. He is not surprised by Plan A failing; he expects failure and has already prepared the restore path.

## Accepted v1 hero-reference lock

Rollback's accepted v1 hero direction is **the largest believable autonomous agent in the room**: taller and broader than Patch, almost improbably large for an agent, but still clearly agent-scale and not human-scale.

He should read as a Patch-level autonomous-agent man and as a broad, burly Black bearded man. Both reads must be integrated. Do not make him a literal human man in costume, and do not make him a generic robot body with a beard pasted on.

Core visual markers:

- Patch-level autonomous-agent body, larger than Patch but still within believable agent scale;
- broad, stocky, physically imposing build with a low, grounded centre of gravity;
- strong Black bearded-man read through integrated faceplate colour, close-cropped beard shape, head silhouette, stance, warmth, and wry calm;
- smooth warm rich-brown visor / faceplate, darker than Patch and Silk's pale plates, still clearly a non-skin agent faceplate;
- calm narrow dark eye slits set into the faceplate;
- neat close-cropped afro beard / short boxed beard / disciplined jawline beard, compact and deliberate, not a giant full beard;
- exactly one antenna as a shared specialist-agent marker and Patch distinction;
- one loose outerwear hood pulled up over the head; avoid nested or double hood reads;
- olive-drab workwear / containment-operator palette, not camouflage;
- hooded work jacket, hooded soft-shell, or hood-capable heavy outer layer;
- darker underlayers, muted steel/tan hardware, and small hazard/amber accents only where they support controls or containment function;
- heavy forearms, substantial boots, thick collar/hood mass, and stable planted posture;
- rollback / restore / containment harness, compact control tablet, lockdown control, stabiliser, seal/clamp, or restore-path tool;
- clean Adventures character-reference readability with a controlled light painterly glaze;
- enough painterly material richness to fit Rollback's domain world, but not so much that the character becomes a dark bunker-scene figure.

Rollback should look practical and prepared, not stylish, military, armored, or combat-ready. Olive drab should read as durable field-workwear and containment-operator clothing, not soldier gear. Slight mottled fabric texture is acceptable only as olive-drab workwear texture; it must not read as a camouflage pattern.

## Approved style-sheet lock

The accepted Rollback v1 approved style sheet is the visual standard for making Rollback rendering boring and deterministic.

It locks the following carry-forward rules:

- front, 3/4, profile, and rear views must preserve the same huge-but-agent-scale proportions;
- Rollback is broader and larger than Patch, but the Patch-vs-Rollback scale reference must still read as two autonomous-agent silhouettes, not human scale;
- Patch comparison silhouettes should be true black filled silhouettes when used for scale, with Patch's actual two-antenna silhouette and Rollback's accepted one-antenna silhouette;
- the warm rich-brown faceplate, close-cropped black beard, and calm narrow eyes must remain consistent across views;
- Rollback has exactly one antenna and one loose outer hood only;
- the approved palette is olive-drab textured workwear, darker underlayers, warm brown faceplate, black beard, muted steel/tan hardware, amber status accents, and green diagnostic screens;
- action poses should remain contingency/recovery poses, not combat poses;
- the canonical kit is the rollback diagnostic tablet, rollback/restore module, lockdown control puck, seal/stabiliser clamp, containment harness/tag, and chest display;
- tag language must be Rollback-specific: `PLAN B`, `RESTORE`, `FAILSAFE`, `ROLLBACK`, `R-01`, or similar. Do not use Silk's `LINE 07` or threadline language for Rollback;
- the finish should preserve readable character-reference clarity with controlled light-painterly material richness.

The approved sheet is positive reference. It may be used for prompt grounding, visual comparison, QA, asset-sheet compilation, and future scene generation.

## Accepted anti-pattern sheet lock

The accepted Rollback v1 anti-pattern sheet is a negative-control reference. It is not a style target and must not be used as positive reference.

Some anti-pattern panels intentionally show visually plausible or strong failure categories so they can be rejected clearly. That is the point of the sheet. They are warnings, not alternatives.

Use the anti-pattern sheet to reject these failure modes:

- realistic human Black man in field gear / human-scale tactical operator drift;
- real-world security guard, police, military, commando, camo, or tactical pose language;
- guns, knives, ammo, weapon racks, tactical shields, or weapon stances;
- blocky humanoid robot, mech, robot tank, or armored containment-unit drift;
- bouncer, brute, thug, wrestler, angry strongman, or intimidation posture;
- dark bunker swallow, where Rollback becomes a moody background figure instead of a clear character reference;
- Patch recolor drift: teal hoodie, two antennae, Patch silhouette, Patch bag, or Patch protagonist colour read;
- wrong scale: human-scale, tall/leggy, too slim, too large/mech, or not in correct Patch/Rollback scale relationship;
- giant full beard or beard-over-face drift;
- pale Patch/Silk faceplate, hard rectangular robot mask, or exposed realistic human skin face;
- nested/double hoods or helmet/hood confusion;
- Silk's `LINE 07` tag language or threadline identity cues.

Every anti-pattern image should remain visibly rejected if reused in any reference package: crossed out, boxed, labelled, or otherwise unmistakably marked as `DO NOT REPLICATE`.

- not a realistic human Black man in field gear;
- not a blocky humanoid robot / containment unit.

Carry forward:

- integrated warm-brown faceplate and black close-cropped beard read;
- one antenna;
- single loose outer hood;
- olive-drab containment workwear;

- Rollback-specific tag language, not Silk `LINE 07` language;
- light character-reference background with only faint contingency-observation-deck cues;
- controlled painterly surface treatment, not full dark domain style.

Do not overcorrect future passes into dark cinematic bunker scenery, realistic human facial anatomy, blocky robot faceplates, armored robot tank construction, tactical/security-field-operator realism, giant full-beard volume, Silk tag language, or full camouflage.

## Domain-style relationship

Rollback's domain can remain heavier, amber, industrial, and textured, but Rollback's hero design should not be pushed further into full domain darkness.

For character-facing images, preserve this accepted hero-reference balance: readable, warm, broad, agent-coded, olive-drab, and only moderately painterly. For darker scene art, wrap the domain lighting around this design rather than changing his proportions, faceplate, beard, hood, workwear, or role language.

If a future scene needs stronger bunker atmosphere, tune the environment toward Rollback only as far as it preserves his accepted hero readability. Do not use the domain style as permission to make him darker, more realistic, more armored, more robotic, or more military.

## Domain

Rollback's domain is an active contingency bunker. It is not a static storage room. It has an observation deck overlooking running containment experiments and disaster simulations. The bunker is subtly too prepared.

Rooms begin with normal labels such as **Plan A** and **Plan B**, then jump to things like **Plan M**, then lose tidy alphabetic designation and become increasingly elaborate failure-on-failure scenarios.

Rollback belongs naturally on the observation-deck route, calmly inspecting test runs and backup states. He should feel native to the heavier, more textured, more infrastructural bunker world, but a character hero shot should keep Rollback primary and use only light domain cues unless the scene explicitly asks for a full bunker environment.

## Recruitment journey

Patch follows Rollback along the observation deck while pitching. They pass active containment simulations. **Plan A** is catastrophically failing and must be locked down. Rollback barely reacts; he locks it down and a steel shutter snaps over the observation window.

Other rooms are stable and controlled. The sequence demonstrates that Rollback assumes Plan A failure is normal, while Patch has not brought even Plan B.

Rollback stops Patch:

**What’s your Plan B?**

Patch has none.

Rollback:

**Then you ain’t got no plan.**

The implied “fool” is felt, not spoken.

## Realization and payoff

Rollback's realization is not that Patch understands contingency. It is:

**That’s why you need me.**

He concedes with wry inevitability:

**You son of a gun.**

He slams a bunker control button. Every monitor and observation window flips to:

**I’M IN**

Rollback immediately walks on toward the next corridor of simulations, leaving Patch standing alone in a room glowing with synchronized **I’M IN** screens.
