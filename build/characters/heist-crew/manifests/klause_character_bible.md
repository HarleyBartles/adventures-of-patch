# Klause Character / Recruitment-Language Bible

State: `accepted_hero_first_pass_visual_anchor_plus_provisional_recruitment_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit recruitment / character-language bible

This character bible locks approved recruitment-language doctrine for planning and pre-vis, and now records the accepted Klause hero first-pass visual anchor. It does not yet lock a full turnaround, final style sheet, final anti-pattern sheet, or complete accepted asset-sheet canon.

## Function

Klause is the decision specialist. He selects the workable route after evidence, pressure testing, authority, and contingency constraints make the option space smaller.

## Character posture

Klause is German, serious, studious, disciplined, and intolerant of wasted time. He does not leave his immaculate desk. He barely moves. His domain is decision discipline, not walking someone through a location.

He should read as exact, composed, and difficult to impress rather than angry, theatrical, punitive, villainous, or authoritarian. His motion is minimal: pen pauses, attention shifts, posture changes, then decision lands.

## Domain

Klause's domain is an immaculate decision office: severe negative space, exact desk geometry, appointment logic, one aligned pen, controlled light, rigid order, and an atmosphere where preparation is evidence of decision-readiness.

The office supports him but must not dominate him. For hero and character-reference imagery, keep the composition character-first, sparse, and readable. Use minimal desk elements: one aligned pen, one pitch binder, and optionally one decision stamp. Avoid dashboards, walls of screens, paperwork sprawl, sticky-note clutter, or consultant/analyst infographics.

## Accepted hero first-pass visual anchor

The accepted Klause hero direction is a hood-up Patch-level autonomous-agent decision specialist seated at an immaculate desk, reading or assessing Patch's pitch binder.

Locked visual traits:

- hood up;
- single antenna;
- muted office-navy / slate-blue zip-through hoodie, open and unzipped;
- crisp white shirt beneath the hoodie;
- dark narrow tie;
- pale grey hands matching the face material, not black gloves;
- restrained, serious posture at the desk;
- sparse desk with one aligned pen and Patch pitch binder;
- clean Adventures editorial-vector / cel-shaded finish, not photorealism or glossy toy rendering;
- character-first framing, not a style sheet unless explicitly requested.

### Face construction lock

Klause's face is a single continuous curved-glass / frosted-screen face surface inside the hood opening.

The face screen is the face. It is not a mask strapped onto a round head. It does not end at the eyes. The place where a human chin would be is still the same continuous screen material. Facial marks, including the eyes and any small mouth mark, must sit on or within this one continuous face-screen surface.

The accepted direction is curved glass / smooth screen, not metal jaw construction. It should avoid both extremes: no plain bobble head with features drawn on, and no robot helmet with a separate mechanical chin.

## Visual separation from other crew members

Klause must not inherit another character's identity language.

- Not Patch: no teal hoodie, no two hood antennae, no crossbody bag, no `>` identity mark, no cheerful protagonist face read.
- Not Silk: no dark charcoal stealth/crawlspace hood read, no threadline inspector equipment, no ninja/assassin mood.
- Not Writ: no moustache, no tweed, no legal chamber palette, no ledger/seal authority props, no judge/lawyer read.
- Not Rollback: no bulky containment-worker mass, no olive utility suit, no Plan B/restore/failsafe body language.
- Not Bit/Bot: no simple helper-robot body, no generic robot mascot ontology.

Klause's role palette is disciplined office navy / slate blue, white, dark tie, pale grey face/hands, restrained charcoal accents, and sparse desk neutrals.

## Recruitment journey

Patch arrives with a pitch binder and begins to pitch.

Klause, initially without looking up:

**Do you have an appointment?**

Then:

**If you don’t have an appointment, I doubt you have a decision. Come back when you're more prepared.**

Patch keeps pitching. Klause listens despite himself.

He later diagnoses:

**This plan has too many options. I’ve been rejecting them while you talk.**

The only workable route is not one Patch has already chosen. It is Klause's decision function.

## Realization and payoff

Klause's “son of a gun” moment reads as:

**Well played, sir. You win at my game.**

Spoken line:

**You son of a gun.**

He gestures for Patch's pitch binder. Patch hands it over. Klause does not open it. He stamps the front cover:

**I’M IN**
