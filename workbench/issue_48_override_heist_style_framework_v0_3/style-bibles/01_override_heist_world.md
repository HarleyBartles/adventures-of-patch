## Source posture

This file decomposes and preserves the global world bible from the combined v0.1 packet, with later style-candidate learnings. It is a global world bible, not a prompt for any specific character, slide, or asset.

## Asset overview

The Override Heist world is a heightened comic-book action-heist layer inside the Adventures of Patch world. It should feel like a stylish crew job, but the story logic is lawful override design, not criminal bypass. The heist language gives energy, structure, and momentum to a dry governance lesson: a smooth override works because the route was properly designed.

## Function and narrative role

The world must make hard-refusal doctrine and lawful override paths visible. It should turn abstract concepts into staged visual systems: protected surfaces, legal routes, pressure tests, authority boundaries, rollback plans, audit trails, route selection, and a final anticlimactic scan.

## Visual style

Use comic-book action-heist staging while preserving Adventures readability. Acceptable additions include panel borders, caption boxes, action cut-ins, sharper graphic shadows, more dramatic camera angles, close-up inserts, motion lines, and montage rhythm.

Keep the finish clean and vector-adjacent. Do not drift into gritty noir, painterly realism, crime-thriller realism, manga overload, photorealism, or glossy 3D.

## Current style-candidate learnings

The leading world direction is the warm safehouse/worldbuilding direction: cosy but operational planning atmosphere, warm lights, readable safehouse geometry, corridors, shelves, lockers, route boards, trays, and controlled lawful-heist cues. The clean access-control direction is useful as protected-surface reference. The blueprint-layer direction is useful as process-language reference. The bold graphic action direction is useful for panel-energy accents, not as the global default.

Avoid making the world default into multi-panel infographic boards with too much embedded text. The world should communicate through lighting, motifs, geometry, access-control surfaces, paper/route artifacts, and panel logic more than through slogans or checklists.

## Palette

Use a cohesive heist palette built around deep navy, charcoal, warm interior light, off-white planning surfaces, controlled teal continuity from Patch, and small semantic accents such as green approval, amber caution, red rejection, blue route logic, and gold/cream authority marks.

Patch teal is protected. Other agents and world systems may use teal only as a minor environmental or interface accent, not as their identity read.

## World materials and motifs

Core motifs:

- vault doors, access lights, keycard readers, route diagrams;
- safehouse corridors, planning tables, blueprints, labelled trays, recovery lockers;
- warm lamps, shelves, workbenches, corridor alcoves, corkboard shapes;
- clean comic panels and readable caption boxes;
- cryptographic strips, audit stamps, mandate seals, rollback tags;
- theatrical heist lighting softened into the Adventures world.

## Text and signage posture

Text may appear as short diegetic labels only: `PLAN B`, `PLAN C`, `AUTHORITY`, `AUDIT`, `ROUTE`, `TEST`, `REJECTED`, `APPROVED`, `VAULT`, `OVERRIDE`, `NO BYPASS`. Avoid long paragraphs, pseudo-code walls, lorem ipsum, decorative serial numbers, or random hacker text.

## Belongs in this asset family

- Clean comic-book heist panels.
- Stylish but friendly action staging.
- Clear lawful-override motifs.
- Functional heist spaces that teach process layers.
- Dramatic lighting that supports readability.
- Anticlimactic success imagery where the final action is simple because the planning was correct.

## Does not belong in this asset family

- Criminal glamour.
- Gun-first heist imagery.
- Bank-robbery clichés as the main read.
- Explosions, chases, violence, or threat as the point.
- Dark noir murk that hides the learning.
- Evil vault framing.
- Generic hacker-in-hoodie aesthetics.
- Illegible comic-page chaos.

## Allowed variation

Safe to vary:

- panel count and panel shapes by scene;
- lighting intensity by story beat;
- accent colours for specialist roles;
- amount of comic texture, as long as readability remains high;
- camera angle, including close-ups, low-angle hero shots, planning-table overheads, and dramatic corridor reveals.

Do not vary:

- lawful override semantics;
- clean Adventures readability;
- Patch identity when Patch appears;
- the rule that the heist is a designed route, not a bypass.

## Prompt-negative canon block

