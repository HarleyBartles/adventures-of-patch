## Asset overview

The crew are Patch-level autonomous-agent specialists. They are not animals, humans, Bit/Bot-style robots, Patch variants, or Patch clones. They may borrow human-like wardrobe, personality, posture, age-coding, profession-coding, and cultural cues, but their visual read must remain autonomous-agent character, not literal human.

## Function and narrative role

Each crew member embodies one layer of lawful override design:

- Index: design history, source evidence, candidate legal routes.
- Silk: adversarial pressure testing and loophole rejection.
- Rollback: recovery, containment, abort conditions, restore route.
- Writ: mandate and authority boundary.
- Klause: decision function and route selection under constraints.
- Receipt: audit trail and durable record.

The crew must be visually distinct enough that each layer can be recognized in montage, planning, and specialist-zone scenes.

## Shared character anatomy / construction

Use an autonomous-agent character construction compatible with Adventures style:

- simplified, readable, expressive bodies;
- rounded or stylised non-human head/face architecture;
- clear role-specific silhouettes;
- simple graphic eyes or face marks, distinct from Patch’s eye/hood/face-panel system;
- wardrobe and props as role cues;
- friendly capable posture, not menace;
- readable scale and body logic.

They can be agentic and person-like without being human. They should feel like peers of Patch in intelligence and agency, not background helper bots.

## Style relationship to Patch

Patch is the protagonist and anchor. Crew members should share the Adventures finish but not Patch’s identity features. They must avoid:

- teal hoodie as main read;
- hood antennae;
- Patch’s off-white face panel architecture;
- Patch’s black oval eye construction as a copied identity system;
- Patch’s crossbody bag;
- `>` symbol;
- Patch’s full silhouette.


## Character bible / frame bible boundary

Crew character identity and recruitment posture belong in `character-bibles/`. Character-specific panel, frame, gutter, and superframe grammar belongs in `frame-bibles/`. For character design sheets, use this crew class bible plus the relevant character bible. For recruitment slides or frame-heavy references, add the global frame bible and the relevant character frame bible.

## Recruitment repetition rule

The six recruitment slides intentionally repeat the same beat: Patch asks, the recruit's domain reveals the missing lawful layer, the recruit's system validates the answer, and the recruit joins. The repetition is the lesson: a lawful agent override requires repeated thought experiments from different angles.

## Palette

Each specialist may have a role accent. Accent colours should help distinguish function, not steal Patch’s teal identity. Keep palettes cohesive with the heist world.

Suggested provisional accents:

- Silk: dark violet, deep red, cable black, laser red accents.
- Index: warm paper cream, archive brown, muted blue, notebook red tabs.
- Writ: tweed brown, cream, brass/gold, muted green or tea amber.
- Klause: graphite, slate blue, black, disciplined red accents.
- Rollback: deep olive, utility grey, warm amber, safety orange as small labels.
- Receipt: warm cream, ledger green, soft grey, timestamp blue, tidy red stamp accents.

## Prompt-positive canon block

```text
Create a specialist crew member for the Override Heist as a Patch-level autonomous-agent character in the Adventures of Patch visual world. The character should be intelligent, expressive, friendly-capable, and role-readable, with a distinct non-Patch silhouette, clean editorial-vector finish, thick rounded outlines, simplified readable shapes, soft controlled shading, and purposeful heist-world detail. Human-like wardrobe and personality cues may be translated into autonomous-agent design language, but the character must still read as an autonomous-agent specialist rather than a literal human, animal, simple bot, or Patch variant. Give the character clear role-specific props, posture, palette accents, and specialist-zone context when relevant.
```

## Prompt-negative canon block

```text
Do not make the crew member a human, animal, literal spider, Bit/Bot-style simple robot, Patch clone, Patch variant, generic helper bot, villain, criminal gang member, or mascot. Do not give the crew member Patch’s teal hoodie, hood antennae, off-white face panel, full Patch silhouette, teal crossbody bag, `>` symbol, or Patch identity palette as the main read. Do not use photorealism, glossy 3D, anime/chibi, gritty noir, horror, gun-heavy action, criminal glamour, or over-detailed sci-fi greebles. Do not let the role become unclear or interchangeable with another crew member.
```

## QA hard gates

- Does the character read as an autonomous-agent specialist, not a human or animal?
- Is the character clearly not Patch?
- Is the character clearly not Bit/Bot-style?
- Is the role visually legible?
- Does the design avoid villain/criminal gang framing?
- Is the character useful to the lawful override process?
- Are props, wardrobe cues, and body language coherent?
- Does the character fit the Adventures style and heist-world styling?
