# Rollback Frame Bible

Version: v0.4  
State: `minimal_provisional_frame_language`  
Operation class: deterministic no-credit frame bible

## Function

Rollback's frame grammar makes recovery, containment, abort conditions, and Plan B logic visible through an active observation-deck route.

## Frame domain

Rollback's panels are chunky, load-bearing observation-window panels. They feel like reinforced bunker architecture: heavy glass, steel shutters, control panels, thick borders, containment doors, hazard margins, and stable infrastructure.

The slide is a linear inspection route along the observation deck. The room progression is the recruitment slide progression.

Rollback's domain should feel like an active contingency bunker, not a static storage room. It is subtly too prepared: every failure has a visible procedure, every window has a shutter, every shutter has a control, and every control implies another fallback.

## Style and material language

Rollback's world is heavier, more textured, more painterly, and more infrastructural than Silk's test-corridor world.

Use:

- worn but maintained reinforced metal;
- thick glass observation windows;
- steel shutter seams;
- hazard margins and amber utility lighting;
- olive, charcoal, warm amber, muted green, steel, and aged industrial surfaces;
- dense control panels, rails, labels, status lights, and containment hardware;
- stable, load-bearing architecture that looks engineered to survive failures.

The space should feel operational and controlled, not chaotic, decorative, or militarised. Warm bunker light and textured painterly heist-comic rendering are preferred when Rollback's domain is shown.

## Observation-route staging

Rollback belongs on a gangplank / observation walkway / control-rail route overlooking running containment tests.

The route should imply inspection in motion. Rollback may be walking, stopping at a control station, calmly observing a chamber, or sealing a failed scenario, but the deck itself should feel like a planned sequence of contingency checks.

For recruitment-slide staging, the room/window order matters:

1. Plan A is visibly failing behind glass.
2. Rollback calmly locks it down and a shutter snaps over the observation window.
3. Later windows show controlled backup simulations.
4. Plan labels escalate from simple Plan A / Plan B / Plan C to elaborate failure-on-failure scenarios.
5. The end state proves that a plan without recovery is not a plan.

## Hero-shot compatibility note

For a Rollback hero-reference image, the full frame sequence is not required. A light but clear suggestion of the observation deck is enough: a reinforced window frame, deck rail, control-panel edge, shutter seam, amber bunker light, hazard stripe, or blurred containment chamber can ground him in the domain.

Do not turn a hero-reference pass into the full recruitment scene. The hero shot should keep Rollback primary while encoding contingency-test inspection energy.

## Motion logic

The frame sequence should show controlled preparedness:

1. Plan A is catastrophically failing behind glass.
2. Rollback calmly locks it down and a shutter snaps over the observation window.
3. Other rooms show controlled backup simulations.
4. Room labels escalate from simple plans to elaborate failure-on-failure scenarios.
5. The end state proves that a plan without recovery is not a plan.

Only Plan A should feel catastrophic. The rest of the route should feel increasingly prepared, controlled, and imaginative rather than increasingly chaotic.
